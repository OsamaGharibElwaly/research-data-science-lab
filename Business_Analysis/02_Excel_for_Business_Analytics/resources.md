## Books

1. Wayne L. Winston, *Microsoft Excel Data Analysis and Business Modeling*. Microsoft Press.
2. John Walkenbach, *Excel 2019 Bible*. Wiley.
3. Michael Alexander and Dick Kusleika, *Excel 365 Bible*. Wiley.

These books cover formulas, data analysis, business modeling, and spreadsheet techniques. Confirm that the edition matches the features available in your version of Excel.

## Suggested learning activities

1. Practice formula references and Tables using a small sales dataset.
2. Rebuild the same analysis using both formulas and PivotTables.
3. Create a dashboard with defined metrics and a visible reporting period.
4. Add data-quality checks and deliberately test invalid records.
5. Recalculate key results independently to validate the workbook.

## Research and further study

For broader study, consult academic library databases for peer-reviewed research on spreadsheet error, data quality, business intelligence, and decision support. Verify article titles, authors, publication details, and DOI links before citing research in a report.

## Resource selection guidelines

Prefer official documentation when checking formula behavior or software compatibility. Test formulas against representative and edge-case data rather than copying unverified examples into a business workbook.

Links and software features can change over time. If a specific Microsoft article is unavailable, search the official Microsoft Support website for the exact function or feature name.
