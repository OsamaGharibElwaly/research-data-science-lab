# Resources — Business Analytics Fundamentals

## 1. Recommended textbooks

1. **Business Analytics: Data Analysis and Decision Making** — S. Christian Albright and Wayne L. Winston. A broad introduction to analytical methods and business decision-making.
2. **Competing on Analytics: The New Science of Winning** — Thomas H. Davenport and Jeanne G. Harris. Explores how organizations use analytics to improve decisions and performance.
3. **Data Science for Business** — Foster Provost and Tom Fawcett. Introduces data-driven reasoning, predictive models, and business value.
4. **The Signal and the Noise** — Nate Silver. Discusses forecasting, uncertainty, and the limitations of predictions.

Use a library or publisher catalog to identify the appropriate edition.

## 4. Professional references

* **CRISP-DM:** A widely used process model for data-mining and analytics projects. Search for the original “CRISP-DM 1.0 Step-by-step Data Mining Guide.”
* **NIST Privacy Framework:** https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework
* **NIST AI Risk Management Framework:** https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework
* **American Statistical Association — Statement on p-Values:** https://www.amstat.org/asa/files/pdfs/P-ValueStatement.pdf

The NIST AI framework is more relevant in later modules that cover AI-enabled decision systems.

## 5. Suggested study order

1. Read the theory file.
2. Identify three business decisions that could benefit from evidence.
3. Practice defining metrics and denominators.
4. Run the Python examples.
5. Complete the exercises before checking the solutions.
6. Write a one-page recommendation for a business problem of your choice.

## 6. Reference management

For any formal portfolio report, record the exact source, title, edition or publication date, URL, access date, and relevant section.

Do not treat a source's existence as proof that a particular claim is correct. Avoid copying proprietary data or copyrighted teaching materials into this repository.
