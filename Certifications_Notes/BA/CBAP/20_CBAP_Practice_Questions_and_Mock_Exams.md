# 20. CBAP Practice Questions and Mock Exams

## 1. Purpose

This module provides original practice questions for CBAP preparation. The questions are designed to test applied business analysis reasoning across the BABOK Guide knowledge areas.

They are not official IIBA questions and do not predict actual examination questions.

## 2. Practice Instructions

For each question:

1. Read the complete scenario.
2. Identify the underlying business analysis problem.
3. Choose the single best answer.
4. Explain why it is better than the alternatives.
5. Record the relevant concept in your weak-area tracker.

For an initial diagnostic, answer all questions before reviewing the answer key.

## 3. Practice Set A: Business Analysis Fundamentals

### Question 1

A stakeholder asks the team to add a dashboard feature. During discussion, the analyst discovers that the real problem is that managers receive inaccurate inventory information. What should the analyst focus on first?

A. Approving the dashboard request
B. Purchasing a new reporting platform
C. Clarifying the underlying business need and investigating the causes of inaccurate inventory information
D. Writing detailed interface requirements immediately

### Question 2

A requirement is clearly written, testable, and internally consistent. However, it does not contribute to any identified stakeholder need or business objective. Which concern is most relevant?

A. The requirement may not be valid for the business need
B. The requirement is automatically approved because it is testable
C. The requirement must be implemented because it is complete
D. The requirement is a project constraint

### Question 3

A project team is selecting an elicitation technique for stakeholders who work in different locations and have limited overlapping availability. Which consideration is most relevant?

A. Always use an in-person workshop
B. Select a technique suited to the objective, stakeholder availability, and context
C. Avoid stakeholder participation
D. Replace all elicitation with document analysis

### Question 4

A business analyst finds that two departments use different definitions of “active customer.” What should the analyst do?

A. Select whichever definition produces the larger customer count
B. Ignore the difference because both departments use the same term
C. Clarify the business purpose and establish an agreed definition or explicitly document contextual differences
D. Delete the metric

### Question 5

A stakeholder says a system must be “easy to use.” What is the most appropriate next step?

A. Treat the statement as a fully testable requirement
B. Clarify the intended users, tasks, context, and measurable usability expectations
C. Replace usability with a technical architecture requirement
D. Reject the requirement without discussion

## 4. Practice Set B: Planning, Governance, and Stakeholders

### Question 6

An analyst joins a project with unclear approval responsibilities. Different managers believe they have final decision authority. What should the analyst do?

A. Allow the most senior person in every meeting to approve all requirements
B. Clarify the governance approach and decision rights with the appropriate stakeholders
C. Let developers determine business priorities
D. Stop all communication until the project ends

### Question 7

A stakeholder has significant influence over funding but limited knowledge of day-to-day operations. Another stakeholder performs the process daily but has no formal approval authority. What is the best approach?

A. Consult only the funding stakeholder
B. Consult only the operational stakeholder
C. Engage both according to their knowledge, interests, and decision responsibilities
D. Ignore the operational stakeholder because they lack approval authority

### Question 8

A project uses an iterative delivery approach. A team member argues that requirements documentation and governance are unnecessary. What is the best response?

A. Agree because adaptive approaches prohibit documentation
B. Explain that documentation and governance should be appropriate to the context and required outcomes
C. Require every possible future requirement to be finalized before work begins
D. Cancel stakeholder reviews

### Question 9

A business analyst must select an approach for a project with uncertain requirements, strict regulatory controls, and multiple external dependencies. What should guide the selection?

A. The analyst's personal preference alone
B. The approach used on the last project, without modification
C. The context, uncertainty, constraints, governance, and stakeholder needs
D. The approach requiring the fewest conversations

### Question 10

A stakeholder repeatedly misses elicitation sessions. The missing information is important to a major design decision. What should the analyst do?

A. Invent the stakeholder's requirements
B. Identify the information gap, explore suitable engagement alternatives, and manage the unresolved decision through governance
C. Remove the stakeholder from all future communications
D. Approve the design without recording the assumption

## 5. Practice Set C: Requirements Life Cycle Management

### Question 11

A proposed change to a requirement affects several downstream requirements and test cases. Which practice is most helpful in assessing the change?

A. Requirements traceability
B. Brainstorming without reviewing dependencies
C. Deleting the original requirement
D. Ignoring downstream impacts

### Question 12

A stakeholder requests a change after the relevant requirements have been approved. What should the analyst do first?

A. Implement the change immediately
B. Reject it because approved requirements can never change
C. Assess the request according to the applicable change and governance process
D. Remove all approval controls

### Question 13

Two requirements conflict. One requires immediate data deletion, while another requires the same data to be retained for a legally mandated period. What should the analyst do?

A. Choose whichever requirement was written first
B. Investigate the applicable legal obligations, business context, and decision authority to resolve the conflict
C. Implement both without considering feasibility
D. Allow the development team to guess

### Question 14

A requirement is approved but has not been communicated to the team responsible for implementation. Which concern is most relevant?

A. Approval alone does not ensure effective communication and lifecycle management
B. Approved requirements must remain confidential from the delivery team in every case
C. The requirement is automatically invalid
D. Traceability is never useful after approval

### Question 15

A team has many requirements but insufficient resources to implement them all in the next release. What should guide prioritization?

A. The order in which stakeholders sent their emails
B. The most senior stakeholder's requests alone
C. Business value, risk, dependencies, urgency, constraints, and available capacity
D. Random selection

## 6. Practice Set D: Strategy Analysis

### Question 16

An organization wants to modernize its systems. Before selecting a technology, what should the analyst establish?

A. The most popular technology in the market
B. The current-state problem, desired future state, business objectives, and relevant constraints
C. The vendor with the largest sales team
D. A detailed implementation schedule without defining the need

### Question 17

A company has strong sales but poor customer retention. Which analytical approach is most useful initially?

A. Assume marketing is responsible
B. Investigate the current state, relevant customer outcomes, business processes, and evidence about retention
C. Replace the sales system immediately
D. Stop measuring customer retention

### Question 18

A proposed future state depends on a new capability that the organization does not currently possess. What should the analyst investigate?

A. The capability gap, required changes, dependencies, and risks
B. Only the color scheme of the new system
C. Whether the gap can be ignored
D. Whether all existing capabilities should be discarded

### Question 19

A strategic initiative no longer supports the organization's revised objectives. What should the analyst recommend?

A. Continue unchanged because the original business case was approved
B. Reassess strategic alignment, costs, benefits, risks, and available alternatives through the appropriate governance process
C. Hide the change from decision-makers
D. Remove outcome measures

### Question 20

An organization has limited resources and six possible initiatives. What is the most appropriate basis for prioritization?

A. Equal funding regardless of value or risk
B. The order in which proposals were submitted
C. Strategic alignment, expected value, risk, dependencies, feasibility, and resource constraints
D. The initiative with the longest documentation

## 7. Practice Set E: Requirements Analysis and Design Definition

### Question 21

A requirement is grammatically correct and contains a measurable acceptance threshold, but it does not address the stakeholder's actual problem. Which distinction is most relevant?

A. Verification versus validation
B. Budget versus schedule
C. Risk versus issue
D. Process versus department

### Question 22

A stakeholder cannot explain a complex approval process clearly. Several exceptions and decision branches exist. Which technique may be particularly useful?

A. A process model showing activities, decisions, and flows
B. A financial statement alone
C. A project title
D. A list of unrelated technical products

### Question 23

A design option meets functional requirements but cannot satisfy a mandatory security constraint. What should the analyst do?

A. Recommend it because functional coverage is sufficient
B. Treat the constraint as a material evaluation factor and assess feasible alternatives
C. Remove the security constraint without authority
D. Ignore the constraint until after deployment

### Question 24

A team has produced several alternative solutions. Which approach best supports a defensible recommendation?

A. Select the first alternative
B. Choose the most expensive alternative
C. Evaluate alternatives against agreed requirements, value, costs, risks, and constraints
D. Select the option preferred by the analyst personally

### Question 25

Stakeholders disagree about whether a requirement is complete. What should the analyst do?

A. Use agreed quality criteria and examine the requirement's purpose, context, and unresolved gaps
B. Approve every requirement immediately
C. Rely only on word count
D. Remove stakeholder review

## 8. Practice Set F: Solution Evaluation, Risk, and Value

### Question 26

A new system is deployed on time, but employees continue using the previous manual process. The expected productivity benefits have not appeared. What should the analyst investigate?

A. Only the deployment schedule
B. Adoption barriers, operational constraints, training, and actual outcome measures
C. Whether more features can be delivered without investigation
D. Whether benefits should be assumed to exist

### Question 27

A risk has a low probability but could cause a major safety incident. What should the analyst do?

A. Ignore it because the probability is low
B. Evaluate the consequences, relevant risk tolerance, obligations, and appropriate escalation
C. Treat it as a realized benefit
D. Remove it from the register

### Question 28

A project has a 25% probability of a $40,000 loss. Under a simple expected-loss model, what is the expected loss?

A. $1,000
B. $10,000
C. $25,000
D. $40,000

### Question 29

An option has a positive ROI but a negative NPV under the selected assumptions and discount rate. What should the analyst do?

A. Ignore the inconsistency
B. Review definitions, timing, cash flows, assumptions, and evaluation methods before recommending a decision
C. Approve the option automatically
D. Remove the discount rate from every future analysis

### Question 30

A solution's performance metric has improved, but customer complaints have increased. What is the most appropriate response?

A. Declare success using only the improved metric
B. Investigate the broader performance evidence, trade-offs, and possible unintended consequences
C. Stop collecting customer feedback
D. Assume the complaints are unrelated

## 11. Scoring and Weak-Area Analysis

This is a practice set of 35 original questions, not an official simulation or a prediction of the CBAP passing score.

For personal practice only, calculate:

$$
\text{Practice Accuracy}=
\frac{\text{Correct Answers}}{35}\times100\%
$$

Use the result to identify revision priorities, not as evidence that you are guaranteed to pass the actual examination.

### Suggested Diagnostic Bands

* **90–100%:** Review explanations and continue with more difficult scenarios.
* **75–89%:** Focus on recurring errors and ambiguous concepts.
* **60–74%:** Revisit relevant BABOK sections and repeat targeted practice.
* **Below 60%:** Rebuild foundational understanding before emphasizing timed practice.

These bands are personal study guidelines, not official IIBA scoring thresholds.

## 13. Additional Scenario Practice

### Scenario A: Conflicting Stakeholder Priorities

Finance wants reduced operating costs. Operations wants faster processing. Both reject the other's proposal.

**Task:** Identify the underlying needs, relevant evidence, decision criteria, and a facilitation approach.

**Suggested reasoning:** Clarify cost and service objectives, establish baseline performance, identify feasible options, assess trade-offs, and use the agreed governance process for unresolved decisions.

### Scenario B: Uncertain Future Requirements

A product team does not yet understand how customers will use a proposed feature.

**Task:** Recommend an analysis and delivery approach.

**Suggested reasoning:** Identify the key assumptions, engage representative users, consider a prototype or limited experiment, define success measures, and refine requirements as evidence develops.

### Scenario C: Solution Underperformance

A system satisfies documented requirements but does not improve the business outcome.

**Task:** Identify possible causes and next steps.

**Suggested reasoning:** Examine whether the requirements addressed the correct need, whether the solution was adopted, whether operational constraints remain, and whether the expected benefits were based on valid assumptions.

### Scenario D: Material Risk

A solution appears financially attractive but introduces a serious security risk.

**Task:** Explain how the analyst should support the decision.

**Suggested reasoning:** Validate the risk, identify mandatory constraints, evaluate mitigation options, assess residual exposure, compare alternatives, and escalate to the appropriate decision-maker.
