## 2. What Is Business Analysis?

Business analysis is the practice of enabling change in an enterprise by defining needs and recommending solutions that deliver value to stakeholders.

Business analysis focuses on understanding:

* The current business situation.
* The problems or opportunities that justify change.
* The needs of stakeholders.
* The desired future state.
* Potential solutions.
* The value expected from implementing those solutions.

Business analysis is not limited to software development. It can be applied to business process improvement, organizational restructuring, digital transformation, operational optimization, product development, and many other areas.

### Example

A retail company experiences frequent stockouts despite having a large inventory.

A Business Analyst investigates sales patterns, purchasing procedures, supplier lead times, and inventory records. The analyst identifies the underlying causes and recommends improvements to inventory planning.

The solution might involve new software, redesigned processes, improved supplier coordination, or a combination of these changes.

The central objective is to solve the business problem rather than simply introduce a new technology.

## 3. The Purpose of Business Analysis

Business analysis helps organizations make informed decisions about change.

Its primary purposes include:

1. Identifying business needs.
2. Understanding stakeholder concerns and expectations.
3. Defining requirements.
4. Evaluating possible solutions.
5. Supporting decisions about priorities and scope.
6. Reducing uncertainty and unnecessary costs.
7. Evaluating whether a solution delivers its intended value.

A successful business analysis effort connects business needs to measurable outcomes.

### Business Need vs. Solution

A business need explains why change is necessary.

A solution describes how that need might be addressed.

Example:

* Business need: Reduce customer waiting time.
* Possible solution: Introduce an online appointment system.
* Desired outcome: Reduce average waiting time from 40 minutes to 15 minutes.

The online appointment system is only one possible solution. The analyst should investigate the underlying need before recommending it.

## 4. The Business Analyst Role

A Business Analyst acts as a bridge between business stakeholders and the people responsible for designing, delivering, or operating a solution.

Typical responsibilities include:

* Investigating business problems and opportunities.
* Identifying stakeholders.
* Conducting interviews and workshops.
* Analyzing business processes.
* Eliciting, documenting, and validating requirements.
* Evaluating alternatives.
* Prioritizing requirements.
* Communicating findings to stakeholders.
* Supporting solution evaluation.

The exact responsibilities vary according to the organization, industry, project, and delivery approach.

### Important Distinction

A Business Analyst does not necessarily make every business decision. The analyst provides evidence, analysis, recommendations, and facilitation so that authorized stakeholders can make informed decisions.

## 5. Business Analysis and Related Disciplines

### Business Analysis vs. Project Management

Business analysis focuses primarily on needs, requirements, solutions, and value.

Project management focuses primarily on organizing and directing work to achieve project objectives within agreed constraints.

A Business Analyst might determine what a new customer portal must accomplish. A Project Manager might coordinate the schedule, budget, resources, risks, and delivery activities.

The roles can overlap, and one person may perform both roles.

### Business Analysis vs. Systems Analysis

Systems analysis focuses on understanding and specifying system behavior, components, interfaces, and technical requirements.

Business analysis has a broader scope and can include organizational, operational, strategic, and technological changes.

### Business Analysis vs. Data Analysis

Data analysis examines data to discover patterns, answer questions, and support decisions.

Business analysis identifies business needs and recommends changes. Data analysis can provide important evidence within a business analysis engagement.

Example: A data analyst discovers that customer cancellations increase after delayed deliveries. A Business Analyst investigates the operational causes and recommends changes to the delivery process.

### Business Analysis vs. Software Development

Software developers implement and maintain software solutions.

Business Analysts help determine the business needs and requirements that the software should satisfy.

Collaboration between these disciplines improves alignment between business expectations and technical implementation.

## 6. Business Analysis Across the Change Lifecycle

Business analysis can take place before, during, and after a project.

### Before Implementation

* Identify the business problem.
* Understand the current state.
* Define desired outcomes.
* Explore possible approaches.
* Assess feasibility and expected value.

### During Implementation

* Clarify requirements.
* Support prioritization.
* Resolve requirement ambiguities.
* Assess proposed changes.
* Validate that the developing solution aligns with stakeholder needs.

### After Implementation

* Evaluate solution performance.
* Measure business outcomes.
* Identify gaps between expected and actual value.
* Recommend improvements.

Business analysis is not necessarily a linear process. Activities may repeat as new information becomes available.

## 7. Common Business Analysis Deliverables

A deliverable is an output produced through an activity or set of activities.

Examples include:

| Deliverable                      | Purpose                                                            |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Business needs statement         | Explains the reason for change                                     |
| Stakeholder register             | Identifies relevant stakeholders                                   |
| Business process model           | Represents activities and workflows                                |
| Requirements document            | Records requirements in an agreed format                           |
| User stories                     | Describe needs from a user's perspective                           |
| Use case                         | Describes interactions with a system                               |
| Requirements traceability matrix | Links requirements to related artifacts and outcomes               |
| Solution evaluation report       | Assesses solution performance                                      |
| Business case                    | Supports a decision by comparing costs, benefits, and alternatives |

Not every initiative requires every deliverable. The appropriate documentation depends on the context and agreed approach.

## 8. The IIBA and BABOK Guide

The International Institute of Business Analysis (IIBA) is a professional association supporting the business analysis profession.

The Business Analysis Body of Knowledge (BABOK Guide) describes generally recognized business analysis knowledge, tasks, techniques, and competencies.

The BABOK Guide organizes business analysis knowledge into six knowledge areas:

1. Business Analysis Planning and Monitoring.
2. Elicitation and Collaboration.
3. Requirements Life Cycle Management.
4. Strategy Analysis.
5. Requirements Analysis and Design Definition.
6. Solution Evaluation.

These knowledge areas are related and may be performed iteratively rather than in a fixed sequence.

The Entry Certificate in Business Analysis (ECBA) is an entry-level IIBA certification intended to demonstrate foundational business analysis knowledge.

**Exam preparation note:** Always compare study notes with the current official ECBA exam blueprint and IIBA guidance. Exam structures and eligibility requirements can change.

## 9. Essential Terminology

**Business need:** A problem or opportunity that motivates change.

**Stakeholder:** An individual, group, or organization that has a relationship with the change, needs, or solution.

**Requirement:** A usable representation of a need, capability, condition, or characteristic that a solution must satisfy.

**Solution:** A specific way of satisfying one or more needs in a particular context.

**Value:** The worth, importance, or usefulness of something to a stakeholder in a context.

**Change:** The transformation of an enterprise in response to a need.

**Enterprise:** An organizational system or collection of organizations pursuing shared goals.

**Context:** The circumstances influencing a change, including organizational structure, regulations, processes, technology, and culture.

## 10. Practical Case Study

### Scenario: University Registration Delays

A university receives complaints because students wait several hours to register for courses.

The university administration proposes purchasing a new registration platform.

A Business Analyst begins by investigating the problem.

Initial findings:

* Several departments use separate registration spreadsheets.
* Course capacity information is updated inconsistently.
* Students must visit multiple offices to resolve registration conflicts.
* Some approvals require manual signatures.
* The registration platform is outdated.

### Analysis

The stated solution is a new platform, but the underlying need is more reliable and efficient course registration.

Possible options include:

1. Standardizing registration procedures.
2. Centralizing course capacity data.
3. Automating approvals.
4. Improving the existing system.
5. Implementing a new integrated platform.

The analyst compares the options against business needs, stakeholder expectations, feasibility, cost, risk, and expected value.

### Success Measures

* Reduce average registration time from 120 minutes to 30 minutes.
* Reduce registration errors by 70%.
* Reduce unresolved registration conflicts by 50%.

These measures help determine whether the selected solution delivers the intended business value.

## 11. Practice Questions

### Question 1

What is the primary purpose of business analysis?

A. To ensure all projects finish on schedule.

B. To enable change by defining needs and recommending solutions that deliver value.

C. To develop software code for business systems.

D. To approve every business decision.

**Correct answer: B**

Explanation: Business analysis focuses on needs, change, solutions, and value. Project scheduling, coding, and formal decision authority may belong to other roles.

### Question 2

A manager requests a new dashboard. What should the Business Analyst do first?

A. Select a visualization library.

B. Purchase a business intelligence license.

C. Understand the business need and the problem the dashboard should solve.

D. Start writing dashboard code.

**Correct answer: C**

Explanation: A requested solution should not be accepted automatically. The analyst first investigates the underlying need, users, and desired outcomes.

### Question 3

Which discipline is primarily concerned with coordinating project work, schedules, and resources?

A. Project management.

B. Data analysis.

C. Software testing.

D. Requirements elicitation.

**Correct answer: A**

Explanation: Project management coordinates delivery work. Business analysis concentrates on needs, requirements, solutions, and value.

### Question 4

Which statement best describes a stakeholder?

A. Only the person paying for the project.

B. Only the person using the final solution.

C. An individual, group, or organization related to the change, need, or solution.

D. Only a member of the project team.

**Correct answer: C**

Explanation: Stakeholders include customers, users, sponsors, managers, regulators, suppliers, operational teams, and other parties relevant to the change.

### Question 5

A company implements a new system but does not measure whether customer complaints decrease. What important activity is missing?

A. Solution evaluation.

B. Source code compilation.

C. Stakeholder identification only.

D. Project scheduling.

**Correct answer: A**

Explanation: Solution evaluation assesses performance and determines whether expected outcomes and value are being achieved.

### Question 6

Which is the best example of a business need?

A. Build a mobile application using React Native.

B. Purchase ten servers.

C. Reduce order-processing errors and improve customer satisfaction.

D. Use PostgreSQL as the database.

**Correct answer: C**

Explanation: This statement describes the desired business outcome without prematurely committing to a particular implementation.
