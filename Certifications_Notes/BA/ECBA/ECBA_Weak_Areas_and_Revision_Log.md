# ECBA Weak Areas and Revision Log

## 1. Purpose

This log helps identify recurring mistakes during ECBA preparation and turn them into targeted revision activities.

The objective is not merely to increase practice scores. It is to understand why an answer was incorrect, correct the underlying misconception, and demonstrate improvement on a later question.

## 3. Error Categories

Classify each incorrect answer using one or more categories.

### Conceptual Gap

You do not understand the underlying concept or its purpose.

**Example:** Confusing requirement verification with requirement validation.

**Corrective action:** Review definitions, compare similar concepts, and explain the difference in your own words.

### Terminology Confusion

You understand the general idea but confuse technical terms.

**Example:** Confusing a business requirement with a stakeholder requirement.

**Corrective action:** Create a comparison table and practice classifying examples.

### Misreading the Question

You overlook a word such as *first*, *best*, *most appropriate*, or *except*.

**Corrective action:** Identify the requested action before evaluating the answer options.

### Premature Solution Selection

You choose an implementation before establishing the need or investigating the problem.

**Example:** Recommending a new application before analyzing the current process.

**Corrective action:** Practice problem analysis, current-state assessment, and evidence gathering.

### Stakeholder Oversight

You ignore a relevant stakeholder, conflict, constraint, or communication need.

**Corrective action:** Identify who is affected, who makes decisions, and whose needs require investigation.

### Weak Scenario Application

You can define a concept but cannot determine when it applies.

**Corrective action:** Solve case studies and explain the relationship between the context and the selected technique.

### Time Pressure

You know the concept but make rushed decisions.

**Corrective action:** Practice timed sets, eliminate clearly unsuitable options, and reserve time to review flagged questions.

### Guessing Without Evidence

You select an answer based on familiarity or intuition rather than the scenario.

**Corrective action:** Identify the evidence supporting the answer and explain why the alternatives are weaker.

## 5. Important Concept Comparisons

### Verification vs. Validation

| Dimension        | Verification                                                  | Validation                                                         |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Main concern     | Quality of requirements or other work products                | Alignment with the need and expected value                         |
| Typical question | Is the requirement clear, complete, consistent, and testable? | Does the requirement or solution address the right need?           |
| Common mistake   | Treating approval as proof of quality                         | Treating a well-written requirement as proof of business relevance |

### Business vs. Stakeholder vs. Solution vs. Transition Requirements

| Requirement Type | Focus                                   | Example                                                    |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Business         | Organizational goal or outcome          | Reduce processing costs by 15%.                            |
| Stakeholder      | A stakeholder's need                    | Warehouse staff need to see current stock levels.          |
| Solution         | A capability or quality of the solution | The system shall display available inventory by product.   |
| Transition       | Temporary capability needed for change  | Existing inventory records must be migrated before launch. |

### Functional vs. Non-Functional Requirements

| Type           | Focus                             | Example                                                                       |
| -------------- | --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Functional     | Behavior or capability            | The system shall create a purchase order.                                     |
| Non-functional | Quality or performance constraint | Standard searches shall complete within two seconds under defined conditions. |

### Need vs. Solution

| Concept  | Meaning                   | Example                                 |
| -------- | ------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Need     | Problem or opportunity    | Customers wait too long for refunds.    |
| Solution | A way to address the need | Introduce an automated refund workflow. |

**Revision rule:** State the need without naming a technology. Then compare multiple possible solutions against the need.

## 8. Spaced Revision Schedule

A suggested personal schedule is:

* **Day 0:** Review the mistake and correct the concept.
* **Day 1:** Explain the concept without notes.
* **Day 3:** Answer a new question on the topic.
* **Day 7:** Revisit the concept in a mixed question set.
* **Day 14:** Confirm retention with a scenario question.

Adjust the intervals according to your schedule and performance.

## 9. Weak-Area Prioritization

Prioritize a topic when one or more of these conditions apply:

1. You repeatedly choose incorrect answers.
2. You cannot explain the concept without notes.
3. You confuse it with a closely related concept.
4. You cannot apply it to a business scenario.
5. It is an important part of the current exam blueprint.
6. Errors persist despite multiple revisions.

A simple priority model can help:

**Priority score = impact × frequency × uncertainty**

Rate each factor from 1 to 3:

* Impact: How consequential is the gap to preparation?
* Frequency: How often does the error occur?
* Uncertainty: How unsure are you about the concept?

The score is a personal planning aid, not an official IIBA scoring method.

## 10. Recovery Plan for a Weak Topic

1. Stop repeating questions without reviewing the concept.
2. Return to the relevant study material.
3. Write a short explanation in your own words.
4. Compare the concept with its nearest alternatives.
5. Create one original example.
6. Answer five to ten new practice questions.
7. Explain the reasoning for every answer.
8. Schedule a spaced reattempt.
9. Update the tracker based on the result.
