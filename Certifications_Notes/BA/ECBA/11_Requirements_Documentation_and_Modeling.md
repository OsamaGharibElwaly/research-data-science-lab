## 2. Business Analysis Fundamentals

### 2.1 What Is Business Analysis?

Business analysis enables change in an enterprise by defining needs and recommending solutions that deliver value to stakeholders.

A Business Analyst investigates problems, identifies opportunities, facilitates communication, analyzes requirements, and supports decisions.

Business analysis is not limited to software development. It can support operational improvement, organizational change, strategic planning, compliance, and technology initiatives.

### 2.2 Essential Terms

**Business need:** A problem or opportunity that motivates change.

**Stakeholder:** An individual, group, or organization with a relationship to the change, its needs, or its outcomes.

**Requirement:** A usable representation of a need.

**Design:** A representation of a solution.

**Solution:** A specific way of satisfying one or more needs within a context.

**Value:** The worth, importance, or usefulness of something to a stakeholder in a context.

**Context:** The circumstances influencing a change, including organizational, technological, regulatory, and environmental factors.

**Change:** The transformation of an enterprise in response to a need.

### 2.3 Example

A delivery company experiences frequent shipment delays.

* Business need: Improve delivery reliability.
* Stakeholders: Customers, dispatchers, drivers, managers, and suppliers.
* Requirement: Customers need access to current shipment status.
* Design: A tracking interface integrated with shipment data.
* Solution: The implemented tracking capability and associated processes.
* Value: Better customer visibility and potentially fewer status inquiries.

The terms are related but are not interchangeable.

## 3. Core Concepts Review

### 3.1 Need, Requirement, and Design

A need explains why change may be necessary.

A requirement describes something needed to address the need.

A design represents a possible way to satisfy requirements.

Example:

* Need: Reduce fraudulent transactions.
* Requirement: Suspicious transactions must be flagged for review.
* Design: A transaction-monitoring component evaluates defined risk indicators.

### 3.2 Stakeholders

Stakeholders may have different goals, authority, expectations, and concerns.

The analyst identifies relevant stakeholders and supports communication and decision-making.

Stakeholder disagreement should be investigated rather than automatically resolved in favor of the most vocal participant.

### 3.3 Value

Value depends on stakeholder priorities and context.

It may include:

* Revenue growth.
* Cost reduction.
* Better customer experience.
* Improved safety.
* Regulatory compliance.
* Reduced risk.
* Better information.
* Operational reliability.

A solution should be evaluated against the outcomes it is intended to achieve.

## 4. Planning and Monitoring Review

Business analysis planning establishes how the analysis work will be performed.

Monitoring examines progress and whether the approach remains appropriate.

Governance establishes decision-making responsibilities, authority, and controls.

Information management addresses how business analysis information is organized, stored, maintained, accessed, and communicated.

Stakeholder engagement planning determines how stakeholders will participate and receive information.

### Key Distinction

A project plan and a business analysis approach are related but different.

A project plan typically coordinates the overall initiative, including schedule, resources, and delivery activities.

A business analysis approach explains how the analysis work will be performed in its context.

## 5. Elicitation and Collaboration Review

Elicitation is the work of drawing out, discovering, and confirming information relevant to business analysis.

Collaboration involves working with stakeholders and other participants to develop shared understanding and achieve outcomes.

Common elicitation techniques include:

* Interviews.
* Workshops.
* Observation.
* Document analysis.
* Surveys.
* Brainstorming.
* Prototyping.

### Confirming Results

Elicitation does not end when a meeting finishes.

The analyst may need to document findings, confirm understanding, resolve inconsistencies, and follow up on unanswered questions.

### Example

An interview reveals that employees regularly correct customer addresses manually.

The analyst should investigate the cause, determine the business impact, and confirm whether the issue is related to data quality, process design, or system functionality.

The analyst should not immediately assume that a new application is required.

## 6. Requirements Life Cycle Management Review

The five tasks are:

1. Trace Requirements.
2. Maintain Requirements.
3. Prioritize Requirements.
4. Assess Requirements Changes.
5. Approve Requirements.

### Common Exam Distinctions

**Traceability:** Establishes relationships between requirements and related information.

**Maintenance:** Keeps requirements and designs accurate, usable, and relevant.

**Prioritization:** Determines relative importance.

**Change assessment:** Evaluates the implications of a proposed change.

**Approval:** Establishes acceptance by the appropriate authority.

### Example

A stakeholder requests a new reporting feature shortly before a release.

The analyst investigates the reason for the request, identifies affected requirements, assesses cost and schedule impacts, and presents the findings to the appropriate decision-makers.

The analyst should not confuse assessing a change with approving it.

## 7. Strategy Analysis Review

Strategy analysis connects the business need with the current state, desired future state, risks, and change strategy.

### Essential Questions

* What problem or opportunity exists?
* What is happening today?
* What outcome is desired?
* What gaps prevent that outcome?
* What risks could affect the change?
* What approach is most suitable?

### Example

A warehouse experiences a high stockout rate.

The current-state analysis identifies inaccurate inventory records.

The future state defines an acceptable inventory accuracy target.

Gap analysis identifies missing data controls and process weaknesses.

Risk assessment examines migration, operational disruption, and adoption risks.

The change strategy compares process improvements, system integration, training, and alternative solutions.

## 8. Requirements Analysis and Design Definition Review

The six tasks are:

1. Specify and Model Requirements.
2. Verify Requirements.
3. Validate Requirements.
4. Define Requirements Architecture.
5. Define Design Options.
6. Analyze Potential Value and Recommend Solution.

### Verification vs. Validation

Verification asks whether requirements and designs are appropriately expressed.

Validation asks whether they address the underlying business need.

### Example

Requirement: "The system should respond quickly."

Verification identifies ambiguity.

After clarification, the requirement specifies an agreed response-time threshold.

Validation then examines whether that threshold is appropriate for the intended users and business objective.

Both activities contribute to requirement quality and relevance.

## 9. Solution Evaluation Review

Solution evaluation assesses actual performance, limitations, and opportunities to improve value.

Important concepts include:

* Performance measures.
* Baselines.
* Targets.
* Actual results.
* Solution limitations.
* Enterprise limitations.
* Improvement recommendations.
* Financial and non-financial value.

### Example

A company introduces automated invoice processing.

The system functions correctly, but invoices still experience delays because managers approve them infrequently.

The analyst should investigate organizational workflow and approval constraints rather than automatically assuming that the software is defective.

## 10. Techniques Review

| Technique         | Main Purpose                                              |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Interview         | Explore stakeholder information and experiences           |
| Workshop          | Collaborate toward a shared outcome                       |
| Observation       | Understand actual behavior and work                       |
| Document analysis | Extract information from existing materials               |
| Brainstorming     | Generate ideas                                            |
| SWOT              | Examine strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats |
| PESTLE            | Analyze external environmental factors                    |
| Five Whys         | Investigate possible root causes                          |
| Process modeling  | Represent activities, decisions, and flows                |
| Data analysis     | Identify patterns and performance issues                  |
| Decision matrix   | Compare alternatives using criteria                       |
| Benchmarking      | Compare performance with a reference point                |

Technique selection should depend on the question being investigated.

## 11. Practice Examination — Set A

Select the single best answer for each question.

### Question 1

A company wants to reduce customer complaints about delayed deliveries. What should the Business Analyst establish first?

A. The programming language for a new application.

B. The business need, current situation, and relevant stakeholder concerns.

C. The final user interface design.

D. The vendor contract.

**Correct answer: B**

Explanation: The analyst should understand the problem and context before committing to a solution.

### Question 2

Which statement best describes a stakeholder?

A. Only a person who pays for a project.

B. Only a software developer.

C. An individual, group, or organization relevant to a change or its outcomes.

D. Only the project manager.

**Correct answer: C**

Explanation: Stakeholders include anyone relevant to the change, its needs, or its outcomes.

### Question 3

A manager requests a new feature, but its business benefit is unclear. What should the analyst do?

A. Accept the request immediately.

B. Investigate the underlying need and expected value.

C. Reject the request because managers cannot propose requirements.

D. Implement the feature without documenting it.

**Correct answer: B**

Explanation: Understanding the need prevents unnecessary or misaligned solution work.

### Question 4

Which activity most directly establishes how business analysis work will be performed?

A. Planning the business analysis approach.

B. Deploying the final solution.

C. Closing every stakeholder account.

D. Retiring all requirements.

**Correct answer: A**

Explanation: The business analysis approach defines how analysis work will be conducted in its context.

### Question 5

A stakeholder repeatedly provides contradictory information. What is the most appropriate response?

A. Select the most recent statement without discussion.

B. Investigate the inconsistency and confirm the stakeholder's intended meaning.

C. Ignore the stakeholder.

D. Immediately replace the solution.

**Correct answer: B**

Explanation: Conflicting information should be clarified before it is treated as an established requirement.

### Question 6

Which task concerns the relative importance of requirements?

A. Requirements traceability.

B. Requirements prioritization.

C. Requirements maintenance.

D. Requirements approval.

**Correct answer: B**

Explanation: Prioritization helps allocate limited resources according to relative importance.

### Question 7

A new regulation may affect several approved requirements. Which activity should help determine the implications?

A. Requirements change assessment.

B. Stakeholder name collection only.

C. Final report formatting.

D. Brainstorming without evaluation.

**Correct answer: A**

Explanation: Change assessment identifies impacts and supports an informed decision.

### Question 8

A requirement is clearly written but does not address the actual business problem. Which activity is most relevant?

A. Validation.

B. Version control only.

C. Meeting scheduling.

D. Status reporting only.

**Correct answer: A**

Explanation: Validation checks alignment with the underlying business need.

### Question 9

Which model is particularly suitable for representing business activities and decision flows?

A. Process model.

B. Contact list.

C. Salary table only.

D. Project invoice.

**Correct answer: A**

Explanation: Process models describe activities, decisions, and flows of work.

### Question 10

An organization wants to compare building a system with purchasing an existing product. What should it do?

A. Select the option with the newest technology.

B. Evaluate alternatives against relevant value, cost, risk, and feasibility criteria.

C. Ignore stakeholder requirements.

D. Avoid examining the current state.

**Correct answer: B**

Explanation: Design alternatives should be compared using criteria relevant to the business need.

## 12. Practice Examination — Set B

### Question 11

A dashboard reports sales accurately, but managers cannot identify why customer retention is falling. What should the analyst consider?

A. Whether the information and measures support the business question.

B. Whether every dashboard should be deleted.

C. Whether reporting frequency is always irrelevant.

D. Whether data quality never matters.

**Correct answer: A**

Explanation: Accurate reporting is not sufficient if the information does not help answer the relevant business question.

### Question 12

Which technique is most appropriate when an analyst wants to understand informal workarounds used by employees?

A. Observation.

B. Market forecasting alone.

C. Budget approval.

D. Organizational branding.

**Correct answer: A**

Explanation: Observation can reveal actual work practices that formal documentation may omit.

### Question 13

A company wants to understand how inflation, legislation, technology, and social trends may affect a strategy. Which technique is appropriate?

A. PESTLE.

B. MoSCoW.

C. Traceability matrix.

D. User story mapping only.

**Correct answer: A**

Explanation: PESTLE organizes external environmental factors.

### Question 14

A system meets its functional requirements but cannot support expected transaction volumes. What is the likely issue?

A. A performance or scalability limitation.

B. A stakeholder attendance issue only.

C. A business objective automatically achieved.

D. A requirements approval success that eliminates all concerns.

**Correct answer: A**

Explanation: A solution can provide the required functions while failing non-functional performance expectations.

### Question 15

Which is an example of a business capability?

A. The ability to manage customer orders.

B. A single meeting invitation.

C. A temporary email message.

D. A file's creation timestamp.

**Correct answer: A**

Explanation: A capability describes what an organization must be able to do.

### Question 16

A project team discovers that users interpret a requirement differently. What should happen next?

A. Clarify the requirement and confirm shared understanding.

B. Allow each user to implement a different interpretation.

C. Ignore the ambiguity until launch.

D. Delete the business objective.

**Correct answer: A**

Explanation: Ambiguity creates inconsistency and should be resolved at the appropriate stage.

### Question 17

Actual performance improves but remains below the target. What should the analyst conclude?

A. No improvement occurred.

B. The solution has improved relative to the baseline, but the target remains unmet.

C. The target has automatically been achieved.

D. All future measurement is unnecessary.

**Correct answer: B**

Explanation: Baselines and targets provide different reference points for evaluation.

### Question 18

Which activity primarily focuses on establishing relationships between requirements and their sources or related artifacts?

A. Traceability.

B. Brainstorming.

C. PESTLE.

D. Estimation.

**Correct answer: A**

Explanation: Traceability links requirements to objectives, sources, designs, tests, and other relevant information.

### Question 19

An organization has limited resources and many competing requirements. Which technique can help categorize requirements into Must, Should, Could, and Won't have this time?

A. MoSCoW.

B. SWOT.

C. Five Whys.

D. PESTLE.

**Correct answer: A**

Explanation: MoSCoW supports prioritization through four commonly used categories.

### Question 20

What is the most appropriate response when a solution's value is lower than expected?

A. Investigate the causes, evaluate limitations, and recommend evidence-based actions.

B. Assume every stakeholder was wrong.

C. Stop collecting performance data.

D. Automatically replace the entire solution.

**Correct answer: A**

Explanation: Solution evaluation should identify the reasons for the gap and support appropriate improvement decisions.

## 15. Common Exam Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Choosing a solution too early

Understand the business need and current situation before selecting a solution.

### Mistake 2: Confusing verification with validation

Verification examines the quality of requirements and designs. Validation examines alignment with the business need.

### Mistake 3: Confusing priority with status

A high-priority requirement is not necessarily approved or implemented.

### Mistake 4: Treating stakeholder requests as automatically valid

Investigate the underlying need and expected value.

### Mistake 5: Assuming all problems require technology

Problems may originate from processes, training, governance, data quality, or organizational constraints.

### Mistake 6: Ignoring context

The most suitable technique depends on the problem, stakeholders, available information, and delivery environment.

### Mistake 7: Selecting an answer that sounds sophisticated rather than relevant

Prefer the option that directly addresses the problem described in the scenario.
