# 12 — ECBA Final Revision Checklist

## 1. Purpose

This document provides a structured final-review checklist for the Entry Certificate in Business Analysis (ECBA).

Use it to:

* Review foundational business analysis concepts.
* Identify knowledge gaps.
* Track revision progress.
* Practice explaining important distinctions.
* Prepare for scenario-based questions.
* Organize your final study sessions.

This checklist is a supplementary study resource, not an official IIBA exam blueprint or substitute for the current ECBA exam syllabus.

## 11. High-Priority Concept Comparisons

Review the following distinctions until you can explain each without referring to your notes.

| Concept A           | Concept B             | Key Distinction                                               |
| ------------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Business need       | Requirement           | Reason for change vs. representation of what is needed        |
| Requirement         | Design                | What is needed vs. a representation of a solution             |
| Stakeholder         | Business Analyst      | Participant or interested party vs. a business analysis role  |
| Elicitation         | Collaboration         | Discovering information vs. working together toward outcomes  |
| Priority            | Status                | Relative importance vs. current management state              |
| Traceability        | Maintenance           | Relationships vs. keeping information accurate and relevant   |
| Verification        | Validation            | Requirement quality vs. alignment with the business need      |
| Current state       | Future state          | Existing conditions vs. desired conditions                    |
| Gap                 | Solution              | Difference to address vs. an approach for addressing it       |
| Solution limitation | Enterprise limitation | Constraint of the solution vs. constraint of the organization |
| Baseline            | Target                | Reference performance vs. desired performance                 |
| KPI                 | Business objective    | Performance measure vs. intended business outcome             |
| Business capability | Business process      | Ability to achieve an outcome vs. activities performed        |
| Risk                | Issue                 | Uncertain event or condition vs. an existing problem or event |

## 12. Scenario-Based Reasoning Checklist

Before selecting an answer to a scenario-based question, ask:

1. **What is the actual problem?** Identify the underlying need rather than accepting the first proposed solution.
2. **Who is affected?** Identify stakeholders and their concerns.
3. **What information is missing?** Determine whether clarification, elicitation, or analysis is needed.
4. **What is the immediate task?** Distinguish planning, elicitation, analysis, change management, approval, and evaluation.
5. **What constraints apply?** Consider time, cost, risk, regulation, and organizational capabilities.
6. **What outcome is desired?** Connect the decision to business value.
7. **What evidence supports the answer?** Prefer evidence-based decisions over unsupported assumptions.
8. **What is the most appropriate next step?** Select the action that addresses the scenario's immediate need.

## 15. Final Knowledge Test

Try answering these without looking at earlier modules.

1. Why should a Business Analyst investigate the underlying need before recommending a solution?
2. How does traceability support impact analysis?
3. What is the difference between prioritizing a requirement and approving it?
4. Why can a requirement be verified but still fail validation?
5. How does gap analysis support strategy analysis?
6. What is the difference between a solution limitation and an enterprise limitation?
7. When would an interview be preferable to observation?
8. Why should performance targets be compared with both baselines and actual results?
9. How can a weighted decision matrix help compare alternatives, and what are its limitations?
10. Why may an initiative need several business analysis perspectives?

### Suggested Answers

**1.** To ensure that the proposed change addresses the actual business problem and delivers relevant value.

**2.** It establishes relationships between requirements and related objectives, designs, tests, and other artifacts, helping identify affected elements.

**3.** Prioritization establishes relative importance; approval records acceptance by the appropriate authority.

**4.** A requirement can be clear and consistent but still fail to address the real business need.

**5.** It identifies the differences between the existing and desired states, helping determine what change is needed.

**6.** A solution limitation concerns the solution's capabilities; an enterprise limitation concerns organizational conditions such as skills, resources, or policies.

**7.** Interviews are useful for exploring individual experiences and perspectives; observation is useful when actual behavior or informal practices must be understood.

**8.** The baseline establishes the starting point, the target defines desired performance, and actual results show what has been achieved.

**9.** A weighted matrix structures comparisons using criteria and weights, but the results depend on the selected criteria, assumptions, and judgments.

**10.** Complex initiatives may involve strategic capabilities, business processes, data analysis, technology implementation, and iterative delivery simultaneously.

## 16. Final Revision Principles

Remember these foundational principles:

* Understand the need before selecting a solution.
* Identify the stakeholders relevant to the change.
* Select techniques according to the context.
* Keep requirements clear, relevant, and traceable.
* Assess changes before making decisions.
* Verify requirement quality and validate business relevance.
* Compare the current state with the desired future state.
* Evaluate solutions using evidence and appropriate measures.
* Consider organizational limitations as well as technical limitations.
* Connect business analysis decisions to stakeholder needs and value.
