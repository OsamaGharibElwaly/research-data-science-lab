# 03 — Business Analysis Planning and Monitoring

## 1. Module Objectives

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

* Explain the purpose of Business Analysis Planning and Monitoring.
* Understand how a Business Analyst determines the appropriate approach to business analysis.
* Identify stakeholders and plan stakeholder engagement.
* Understand business analysis governance.
* Explain information management planning.
* Identify how business analysis performance is assessed.
* Recognize how plans are monitored and adjusted throughout an initiative.

## 2. Introduction

Business Analysis Planning and Monitoring determines how business analysis work will be performed, managed, coordinated, and evaluated.

Planning helps ensure that analysis activities are appropriate for the initiative, stakeholders, organizational environment, and expected outcomes.

Monitoring helps determine whether the planned activities are effective and whether adjustments are necessary.

Planning is not simply producing a schedule. It involves making decisions about:

* What analysis work is required.
* Who should participate.
* Which techniques will be used.
* How requirements and information will be managed.
* How decisions and approvals will be governed.
* How performance will be evaluated.

Plans should be adapted as circumstances change.

## 3. The Planning and Monitoring Knowledge Area

The BABOK Guide identifies five tasks within Business Analysis Planning and Monitoring:

1. Plan Business Analysis Approach.
2. Plan Stakeholder Engagement.
3. Plan Business Analysis Governance.
4. Plan Business Analysis Information Management.
5. Identify Business Analysis Performance Improvements.

Each task addresses a different aspect of organizing and improving business analysis work.

## 4. Plan Business Analysis Approach

### 4.1 Purpose

This task determines the appropriate approach for performing business analysis.

The approach establishes how the analyst will work, which activities will be performed, and how those activities will be coordinated.

Possible approaches include predictive, adaptive, and hybrid approaches.

### 4.2 Predictive Approach

A predictive approach generally emphasizes planning and defining requirements earlier, with formal change management and sequential delivery stages.

Typical characteristics:

* Greater emphasis on upfront planning.
* More detailed documentation where appropriate.
* Formal reviews and approvals.
* Defined delivery stages.
* Changes assessed against agreed baselines.

Predictive approaches may be suitable when requirements are relatively stable, regulatory documentation is important, or changes are costly.

### 4.3 Adaptive Approach

An adaptive approach emphasizes iterative learning, frequent feedback, and evolving requirements.

Typical characteristics:

* Incremental delivery.
* Frequent stakeholder collaboration.
* Regular reprioritization.
* Short feedback cycles.
* Refinement of requirements as knowledge improves.

Adaptive approaches may be suitable when needs are uncertain or the solution must evolve through experimentation.

### 4.4 Hybrid Approach

A hybrid approach combines elements of predictive and adaptive approaches.

For example, an organization may establish a formal budget and compliance framework while delivering software functionality through short iterations.

The correct approach depends on the initiative's risks, complexity, stakeholder availability, uncertainty, organizational standards, and constraints.

### 4.5 Factors Influencing the Approach

The Business Analyst should consider:

* Business objectives.
* Project size and complexity.
* Requirement uncertainty.
* Regulatory constraints.
* Stakeholder availability.
* Delivery deadlines.
* Organizational culture.
* Available tools and resources.
* Risks associated with change.
* Experience with previous initiatives.

### Example

A hospital implementing a regulated patient-record system may require formal approvals, traceability, and extensive documentation.

A small team experimenting with a new customer-facing feature may benefit from iterative development and rapid feedback.

Neither approach is universally superior.

## 5. Plan Stakeholder Engagement

### 5.1 Purpose

Stakeholder engagement planning determines how stakeholders will participate in business analysis.

It identifies stakeholders, assesses their characteristics, and establishes appropriate communication and collaboration methods.

### 5.2 Stakeholder Identification

Potential stakeholders include:

* Business sponsors.
* Customers.
* End users.
* Subject-matter experts.
* Business managers.
* Developers.
* Testers.
* Operations teams.
* Suppliers.
* Regulators.
* Support personnel.

Stakeholder identification should continue throughout the initiative because new stakeholders may emerge.

### 5.3 Stakeholder Analysis

Stakeholder analysis examines factors such as:

* Roles and responsibilities.
* Authority and decision-making power.
* Influence over outcomes.
* Interests and expectations.
* Knowledge and expertise.
* Attitudes toward change.
* Availability.
* Communication preferences.
* Potential conflicts.

Understanding these factors helps the analyst select suitable engagement strategies.

### 5.4 Stakeholder Engagement Methods

| Method                  | Appropriate Use                                               |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Individual interviews   | Exploring detailed needs or sensitive concerns                |
| Workshops               | Building shared understanding among multiple stakeholders     |
| Surveys                 | Collecting input from a large population                      |
| Focus groups            | Exploring opinions from a selected group                      |
| Demonstrations          | Obtaining feedback on a proposed solution                     |
| Document reviews        | Learning from existing policies, procedures, and requirements |
| Regular status meetings | Coordinating activities and resolving issues                  |

The choice depends on the information needed, stakeholder availability, complexity, and cost.

### Example

An analyst designing a new employee onboarding process interviews HR staff, holds a workshop with department managers, and surveys new employees.

These methods provide different perspectives and help reveal inconsistencies between documented procedures and actual experiences.

## 6. Plan Business Analysis Governance

### 6.1 Purpose

Governance defines how decisions about business analysis work and its outputs will be made.

It establishes decision-making responsibilities, review processes, approvals, and change-control arrangements.

Governance questions include:

* Who approves requirements?
* Who can change priorities?
* Who resolves conflicts between stakeholders?
* How are requirement changes evaluated?
* Who accepts the final deliverables?
* What happens when stakeholders disagree?

### 6.2 Governance Components

A governance plan may specify:

* Decision-making authority.
* Approval responsibilities.
* Review procedures.
* Change-control processes.
* Escalation paths.
* Prioritization authority.
* Documentation standards.
* Reporting requirements.

Governance should be clear enough to prevent confusion without creating unnecessary bureaucracy.

### 6.3 Example

A financial organization requires compliance approval for changes affecting customer data.

The governance arrangement may require:

1. The Business Analyst to document the proposed requirement.
2. The product owner to assess business priority.
3. The compliance team to evaluate regulatory implications.
4. The designated authority to approve the change.
5. The team to update affected requirements and traceability records.

The analyst supports the process but does not automatically possess approval authority.

## 7. Plan Business Analysis Information Management

### 7.1 Purpose

This task determines how business analysis information will be captured, stored, organized, maintained, accessed, and reused.

Business analysis information includes requirements, designs, stakeholder information, decisions, models, assumptions, and other analytical outputs.

### 7.2 Information Management Considerations

A plan may define:

* Documentation formats.
* Naming conventions.
* Version control.
* Storage locations.
* Access permissions.
* Confidentiality requirements.
* Traceability practices.
* Retention periods.
* Review and approval status.
* Reuse of existing information.

Information should remain accessible to authorized stakeholders and protected from unauthorized access.

### 7.3 Traceability

Traceability establishes relationships between related pieces of business analysis information.

For example:

Business objective → Business requirement → Stakeholder requirement → Solution requirement → Test case → Outcome measurement.

Traceability supports:

* Impact analysis.
* Change assessment.
* Coverage analysis.
* Verification.
* Compliance.
* Understanding why a requirement exists.

### 7.4 Example

A company maintains requirements in a shared repository with unique identifiers and version history.

When a regulation changes, the analyst can identify affected requirements, designs, tests, and processes.

This reduces the risk of overlooking related work.

## 8. Identify Business Analysis Performance Improvements

### 8.1 Purpose

This task assesses business analysis work to identify opportunities for improvement.

The focus is on how effectively and efficiently business analysis activities are performed.

It is different from evaluating whether the final business solution delivers value, which is addressed under Solution Evaluation.

### 8.2 Performance Measures

Possible measures include:

* Time required to obtain requirement approvals.
* Number of requirement-related defects.
* Frequency of rework caused by unclear requirements.
* Stakeholder satisfaction with analysis activities.
* Percentage of requirements with agreed acceptance criteria.
* Time required to resolve requirement conflicts.
* Completeness of traceability.
* Effectiveness of workshops.

Measures should be selected carefully. A high number of documents or meetings does not necessarily indicate effective business analysis.

### 8.3 Improvement Cycle

A practical improvement cycle is:

1. Define the desired performance.
2. Collect performance information.
3. Compare actual performance with expectations.
4. Identify causes of gaps.
5. Recommend improvements.
6. Implement changes.
7. Measure results.

### Example

A team repeatedly discovers missing requirements during testing.

The analyst reviews previous projects and finds that key operational stakeholders were not involved early enough.

The team changes its stakeholder engagement plan and introduces an operations review before requirement approval.

The improvement is evaluated by tracking requirement-related defects in subsequent releases.

## 9. Monitoring and Adapting the Plan

Business analysis plans should be reviewed as new information becomes available.

Reasons to adjust a plan include:

* A stakeholder becomes unavailable.
* New regulations introduce additional requirements.
* The delivery approach changes.
* The business priority changes.
* A major assumption proves incorrect.
* A technique produces insufficient information.
* Risks or constraints change.

The analyst should assess the impact of proposed adjustments and follow the applicable governance process.

A plan is a guide for effective work, not a guarantee that the original assumptions will remain valid.

## 10. Practical Case Study

### Scenario: E-Commerce Checkout Improvement

An online retailer wants to reduce checkout abandonment.

The organization has:

* A product owner.
* Customer service representatives.
* Developers.
* A payment provider.
* A compliance team.
* Marketing analysts.

### Planning Decisions

**Approach:** Use iterative delivery because the team can test improvements with users and analyze conversion data frequently.

**Stakeholder engagement:** Interview customer service staff, analyze customer feedback, and conduct usability sessions.

**Governance:** The product owner prioritizes business requirements, while compliance reviews changes affecting payment and personal data.

**Information management:** Store requirements, design decisions, and test results in a version-controlled repository.

**Performance improvement:** Track requirement rework, approval delays, and stakeholder feedback after each release.

### Example Measures

* Reduce checkout abandonment from 65% to 50%.
* Reduce requirement-related rework by 25%.
* Obtain stakeholder review within three business days on average.

These targets are illustrative and should be validated against baseline data and organizational feasibility.

## 11. Practice Questions

### Question 1

What is the main purpose of planning the business analysis approach?

A. To select the final software programming language.

B. To determine how business analysis work will be performed.

C. To approve the organization's annual budget.

D. To guarantee that requirements never change.

**Correct answer: B**

Explanation: The approach determines how analysis activities will be organized and performed based on the initiative's characteristics.

### Question 2

Requirements are uncertain, and the team needs frequent feedback from users. Which approach is generally most suitable?

A. Adaptive.

B. Strictly predictive in every circumstance.

C. No formal approach.

D. A documentation-only approach.

**Correct answer: A**

Explanation: Adaptive approaches support iterative learning, feedback, and evolving requirements.

### Question 3

Who should be considered when planning stakeholder engagement?

A. Only the project sponsor.

B. Only people who will use the final software.

C. All relevant individuals and groups affected by or involved in the change.

D. Only technical team members.

**Correct answer: C**

Explanation: Stakeholders can include customers, operational teams, regulators, suppliers, and other relevant parties.

### Question 4

Which activity is most directly associated with business analysis governance?

A. Defining who approves requirement changes.

B. Writing application source code.

C. Performing database backups.

D. Selecting employee vacation dates.

**Correct answer: A**

Explanation: Governance establishes decision-making authority, approval responsibilities, and change-control procedures.

### Question 5

A team cannot determine which requirements are affected by a new regulation. Which planning area is most directly relevant?

A. Information management.

B. Office scheduling.

C. Software branding.

D. Recruitment planning.

**Correct answer: A**

Explanation: Effective information management and traceability help identify relationships between requirements and related artifacts.

### Question 6

Which measure is most directly associated with improving business analysis performance?

A. Number of office computers.

B. Number of requirements-related defects caused by ambiguity.

C. Color of the application interface.

D. Number of employees in an unrelated department.

**Correct answer: B**

Explanation: Requirement-related defects can reveal weaknesses in analysis practices and help identify improvement opportunities.

### Question 7

A key stakeholder becomes unavailable during elicitation. What should the analyst do?

A. Ignore the stakeholder permanently.

B. Continue without assessing the impact.

C. Review the plan, evaluate the impact, and adjust engagement activities as necessary.

D. Cancel all business analysis work automatically.

**Correct answer: C**

Explanation: Monitoring allows the analyst to adapt the plan to changing circumstances while preserving the initiative's objectives.
