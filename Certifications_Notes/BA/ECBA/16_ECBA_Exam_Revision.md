# 16 — ECBA Full Practice Exam and Answer Explanations

## 1. Purpose

This module provides a longer, original practice examination covering foundational business analysis concepts.

It is designed to test:

* Business analysis terminology.
* Stakeholder engagement.
* Planning and monitoring.
* Elicitation and collaboration.
* Requirements life cycle management.
* Strategy analysis.
* Requirements analysis and design definition.
* Solution evaluation.
* Business analysis techniques.
* Business process modeling.
* Scenario-based decision-making.

**Important:** This is a supplementary practice examination, not an official IIBA exam or a replica of its question bank. Its question count and timing are study choices, not claims about the current official ECBA exam format.

Before the real examination, consult IIBA's current ECBA exam blueprint and candidate guidance.

## 2. Instructions

* Total questions: 40.
* Recommended practice time: 60 minutes.
* Select one best answer per question.
* Attempt all questions before consulting the answer key.
* Record uncertain answers for later review.
* Read the explanations after completing the test.

Suggested scoring:

* 36–40: Strong performance on this practice set.
* 32–35: Good foundation; review missed concepts.
* 26–31: Additional revision recommended.
* 20–25: Revisit foundational modules and repeat the test.
* Below 20: Focus on understanding core terminology before attempting another full practice set.

These are informal study bands, not official IIBA passing thresholds.

## 3. Questions

### Question 1 — Business Analysis Fundamentals

A retailer reports declining customer satisfaction and asks for a new mobile application. What should the Business Analyst do first?

A. Select a mobile development framework.

B. Investigate the underlying problem, stakeholder needs, and business objectives.

C. Approve the application budget.

D. Create a detailed user interface immediately.

### Question 2 — Business Needs

Which statement best describes a business need?

A. A particular database table.

B. A problem or opportunity that motivates change.

C. A specific programming language.

D. A completed test case.

### Question 3 — Stakeholders

Which person may be a stakeholder in a warehouse automation initiative?

A. Only the executive sponsor.

B. Only the software developer.

C. Warehouse employees, customers, managers, technical staff, and other relevant parties.

D. Only the person who pays for the equipment.

### Question 4 — Value

Which statement about business value is most accurate?

A. Value always means immediate financial profit.

B. Value depends on the stakeholder and context and may be financial or non-financial.

C. Value is identical to project cost.

D. Value cannot be measured.

### Question 5 — Planning

Why should the Business Analyst define an appropriate business analysis approach?

A. To determine how analysis work will be performed in the specific context.

B. To guarantee that requirements will never change.

C. To eliminate stakeholder participation.

D. To replace all project planning.

### Question 6 — Governance

A proposed requirement change requires approval from an authorized decision-maker. What should the analyst do?

A. Approve the change independently regardless of authority.

B. Follow the agreed governance and decision-making process.

C. Implement the change before assessing its impact.

D. Delete the original requirement.

### Question 7 — Elicitation

An analyst needs to understand why employees repeatedly correct customer records. Which initial approach is most suitable?

A. Investigate through interviews, observation, and relevant data as appropriate.

B. Purchase a new system immediately.

C. Assume employees are responsible.

D. Ignore the problem.

### Question 8 — Workshops

When is a workshop especially useful?

A. When several stakeholders must collaborate on a shared problem or outcome.

B. When no objectives can be identified.

C. When stakeholders must never communicate.

D. When decisions should remain undocumented.

### Question 9 — Observation

What is a major benefit of observing a business process?

A. It guarantees that all employees behave identically.

B. It can reveal actual work practices and informal workarounds.

C. It eliminates the need for other evidence.

D. It automatically identifies the final solution.

### Question 10 — Collaboration

Two stakeholders provide conflicting descriptions of a business rule. What should the analyst do?

A. Accept the first statement.

B. Accept the most recent statement without investigation.

C. Clarify the conflict, investigate the intended rule, and follow appropriate decision-making procedures.

D. Ignore the requirement.

### Question 11 — Traceability

What is a principal benefit of requirements traceability?

A. It prevents all changes.

B. It helps identify relationships and assess the impact of changes.

C. It automatically approves requirements.

D. It replaces testing.

### Question 12 — Prioritization

Which technique uses the categories Must have, Should have, Could have, and Won't have this time?

A. PESTLE.

B. SWOT.

C. MoSCoW.

D. Five Whys.

### Question 13 — Change Assessment

A regulation changes and may affect several requirements. What should the analyst do?

A. Assess the implications and affected elements before the appropriate decision is made.

B. Immediately delete all related requirements.

C. Ignore the regulation until deployment.

D. Approve every possible change automatically.

### Question 14 — Requirement Approval

What does requirements approval primarily establish?

A. Relative importance.

B. Formal acceptance by the appropriate authority.

C. Technical implementation.

D. A guarantee of business success.

### Question 15 — Current State

What is the main purpose of current-state analysis?

A. To understand existing conditions, capabilities, problems, and relevant constraints.

B. To choose a solution without evidence.

C. To avoid understanding the existing process.

D. To guarantee that the current process remains unchanged.

### Question 16 — Future State

A company wants to reduce invoice processing time from eight days to three days. Which statement describes a future-state objective?

A. Employees currently wait for approvals.

B. The process uses several spreadsheets.

C. Invoice processing should achieve the agreed three-day target under defined conditions.

D. Historical processing time was eight days.

### Question 17 — Gap Analysis

What does gap analysis identify?

A. The difference between the current state and desired future state.

B. The number of employees in a company only.

C. The final source-code structure.

D. The status of a meeting invitation.

### Question 18 — Risk Analysis

A proposed system migration could cause a temporary service interruption. How should the analyst treat this possibility?

A. As a risk requiring appropriate assessment and response.

B. As an issue that has necessarily already occurred.

C. As proof that migration must be abandoned.

D. As irrelevant to business analysis.

### Question 19 — Verification

A requirement states, "The application should be easy to use." What is a primary verification concern?

A. The requirement may be too vague to assess consistently.

B. The requirement automatically proves business value.

C. The requirement has already been implemented.

D. The stakeholder must be ignored.

### Question 20 — Validation

A well-written feature does not contribute to the intended business objective. Which activity is most relevant?

A. Validation.

B. Version numbering only.

C. Meeting scheduling.

D. File compression.

### Question 21 — Functional Requirements

Which is a functional requirement?

A. The system shall support an agreed availability target.

B. Users shall be able to submit purchase orders.

C. The system shall meet an agreed response-time target.

D. The application shall meet an accessibility standard.

### Question 22 — Non-Functional Requirements

Which is an example of a non-functional requirement?

A. Users can create an account.

B. Managers can approve invoices.

C. The application shall achieve the agreed response-time threshold under defined load conditions.

D. The system generates a purchase order.

### Question 23 — Design Options

A company must choose between building a custom application and purchasing an existing product. What is the most appropriate approach?

A. Always choose the cheaper option without further analysis.

B. Compare alternatives using relevant business value, cost, risk, feasibility, and constraints.

C. Always build custom software.

D. Avoid consulting stakeholders.

### Question 24 — Business Rules

What is the primary purpose of a business rule?

A. To define a condition, constraint, or policy that governs business behavior.

B. To describe only the color of an interface.

C. To replace every stakeholder need.

D. To eliminate the need for decision-making.

### Question 25 — Solution Evaluation

A solution improves performance compared with the baseline but fails to achieve its target. What should the analyst conclude?

A. No improvement occurred.

B. Performance improved, but the target remains unmet and may require further analysis.

C. The target was achieved automatically.

D. All future measurement is unnecessary.

### Question 26 — Enterprise Limitations

A system works correctly, but employees lack the training required to use it effectively. What is the primary concern?

A. An enterprise limitation related to skills and adoption.

B. A guaranteed database failure.

D. A version number.

### Question 27 — SWOT

### Question 28 — PESTLE

A. MoSCoW.

### Question 29 — Root-Cause Analysis

A. It helps investigate underlying causes rather than treating only symptoms.

B. It guarantees that no process changes are needed.

### Question 30 — Decision Analysis

A. It cannot compare alternatives.

B. Its results depend on the selected criteria, weights, and judgments.

C. It eliminates uncertainty.

D. It always produces the objectively perfect decision.

### Question 31 — Business Intelligence

A manager needs revenue reports segmented by region, product, and month. Which perspective is especially relevant?

A. Business Intelligence.

B. Business Process Management only.

C. Recruitment.

D. Facilities maintenance.

### Question 32 — Agile

What is characteristic of an Agile delivery approach?

A. Requirements can never change.

B. Work may be delivered incrementally, with feedback informing subsequent decisions.

C. Stakeholders cannot participate.

D. Documentation is always prohibited.

### Question 33 — Business Architecture

What does a business capability primarily describe?

A. What an organization needs to be able to do.

B. A single meeting.

C. The exact sequence of every activity in a process.

D. A particular file format.

### Question 34 — Business Process Management

### Question 35 — Process Modeling

### Question 36 — Bottleneck Analysis

### Question 37 — Performance Measurement

### Question 39 — Conflict Resolution

Two departments want incompatible features, but the available budget cannot support both. What should the analyst do?

A. Automatically favor the department with more employees.

## 5. Answer Explanations

### Questions 1–10

**1. B — Investigate the need.** A proposed mobile application is a possible solution, not proof that the underlying problem requires an application.

**2. B — Business need.** A need describes the problem or opportunity motivating change.

**3. C — Relevant parties.** Stakeholders include those who affect or are affected by the initiative, subject to the context.

**4. B — Context-dependent value.** Value may be financial or non-financial and can differ among stakeholders.

**5. A — Appropriate analysis approach.** Planning establishes how analysis work should be conducted for the initiative.

**6. B — Governance.** Decisions should follow agreed authority and governance arrangements.

**7. A — Investigate before prescribing.** Interviews, observation, and data analysis can help identify underlying causes.

**8. A — Shared collaboration.** Workshops help participants exchange information and work toward a common outcome.

**9. B — Actual behavior.** Observation can reveal practices and workarounds that formal procedures omit.

**10. C — Clarify the conflict.** Inconsistent stakeholder information must be investigated and resolved through appropriate processes.

### Questions 11–20

**11. B — Relationships and impact analysis.** Traceability helps determine which related elements may be affected by changes.

**12. C — MoSCoW.** The method categorizes items into Must, Should, Could, and Won't have this time.

**13. A — Assess impacts.** Regulatory changes may affect requirements, processes, controls, and designs.

**14. B — Formal acceptance.** Approval establishes acceptance by the appropriate authority.

**15. A — Understand the current state.** Analysis of existing conditions supports informed change decisions.

**16. C — Future-state target.** The three-day objective describes the desired outcome, assuming the target is properly defined.

**17. A — Current-to-future gap.** Gap analysis identifies differences between present conditions and desired outcomes.

**18. A — Risk.** A possible future service interruption is an uncertainty to assess and manage.

**19. A — Ambiguity and testability.** "Easy to use" may require measurable criteria and clarification.

**20. A — Validation.** Validation examines whether a requirement or design addresses the business need.

### Questions 21–30

**21. B — Functional requirement.** Submitting purchase orders describes system behavior.

**22. C — Non-functional requirement.** Response time describes a performance characteristic.

**23. B — Compare alternatives.** Business value, cost, risk, feasibility, and constraints should inform the decision.

**24. A — Business rule.** A business rule governs behavior, decisions, conditions, or constraints.

**25. B — Improved but below target.** Actual results should be assessed against both the baseline and desired performance.

**26. A — Enterprise limitation.** Insufficient training is an organizational constraint rather than necessarily a technical defect.

**27. D — Threat.** SWOT threats are external unfavorable factors.

**28. B — PESTLE.** PESTLE provides a framework for external environmental analysis.

**29. A — Underlying causes.** Root-cause analysis investigates why a problem occurs.

**30. B — Judgment-dependent results.** A weighted matrix is only as useful as its criteria, weights, evidence, and assumptions.

### Questions 31–40

**31. A — Business Intelligence.** BI addresses reporting, data, metrics, and analytical decision support.

**32. B — Iterative delivery.** Agile approaches support incremental work, feedback, and adaptation.

**33. A — Organizational ability.** A capability describes what the organization needs to be able to do.

**34. A — Business Process Management.** BPM addresses process analysis, performance, and improvement.

**35. A — Process control flow.** BPMN gateways control branching, merging, and synchronization.

**36. B — Inspection.** Its capacity is lower than that of order preparation, making it a likely constraint.

**37. A — Throughput.** Throughput measures completed work over a defined period.

**38. A — Audience-appropriate communication.** Different stakeholders need different information and levels of detail.

**39. B — Evidence-based trade-offs.** The analyst supports a decision based on needs, alternatives, constraints, and appropriate authority.

**40. B — Integrated analysis.** The problem spans data quality, processes, stakeholder needs, and solution evaluation.

## 7. Final Exam Technique

### Step 1: Identify the Main Concern

Determine whether the question concerns a need, stakeholder, requirement, decision, change, process, or solution outcome.

### Step 2: Identify the Immediate Task

Is the analyst expected to investigate, clarify, model, assess, prioritize, validate, communicate, or evaluate?

### Step 3: Check Constraints

Consider authority, business objectives, dependencies, cost, risk, and available evidence.

### Step 4: Eliminate Weak Answers

Be cautious of answers that:

* Select a solution before understanding the problem.
* Ignore relevant stakeholders.
* Make decisions outside the analyst's authority.
* Skip impact analysis.
* Assume a symptom establishes its cause.
* Guarantee success without evidence.

### Step 5: Select the Best Answer

Choose the action that best fits the specific context and the immediate purpose of the question.
