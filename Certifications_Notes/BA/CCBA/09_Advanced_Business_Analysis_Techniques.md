# 09 — Business Analysis Perspectives

## 1. Introduction

A business analysis perspective is a way of viewing business analysis work within a particular organizational context. Different perspectives influence the types of problems analyzed, the techniques used, the stakeholders involved, and the value expected from a change.

The BABOK Guide describes five perspectives:

1. Agile.
2. Business Intelligence.
3. Information Technology.
4. Business Architecture.
5. Business Process Management.

These perspectives are not mutually exclusive. An organization may use several at the same time.

For CCBA preparation, understand how each perspective changes the focus of business analysis rather than memorizing definitions alone.

## 2. The Agile Perspective

The Agile perspective emphasizes delivering value through iterative development, frequent feedback, collaboration, and adaptation to changing requirements.

### Characteristics

* Frequent stakeholder collaboration.
* Incremental delivery.
* Continuous prioritization.
* Short feedback cycles.
* Lightweight documentation where appropriate.
* Adaptation based on learning.
* Focus on working solutions and measurable outcomes.

### Business analyst responsibilities

* Understand the product vision.
* Elicit and refine requirements continuously.
* Facilitate backlog refinement.
* Support prioritization.
* Define acceptance criteria.
* Help stakeholders evaluate increments.
* Identify dependencies and risks.
* Communicate emerging findings.

### Example

An online retailer wants to improve checkout conversion.

Instead of defining every interface detail before implementation, the team may:

1. Identify the largest checkout problems.
2. Prioritize a small improvement.
3. Build and test the improvement.
4. Analyze customer behavior.
5. Gather feedback.
6. Refine the backlog.

Agile does not mean the absence of planning, documentation, governance, or quality controls. It changes how these activities are organized.

## 3. The Business Intelligence Perspective

Business Intelligence (BI) focuses on turning data into information that supports operational and strategic decisions.

### Common activities

* Identify reporting and analytical needs.
* Define business metrics.
* Assess data sources.
* Establish data quality rules.
* Model data.
* Define dashboards and reports.
* Analyze trends.
* Validate calculations.
* Support decision-making.

### Key concepts

**Data:** Recorded observations or facts.

**Information:** Data organized and interpreted in a useful context.

**Metric:** A defined quantitative measure.

**KPI:** A measure selected to monitor performance against an important objective.

**Dashboard:** A visual presentation of selected measures and information.

### Example

A distributor wants to understand why profits are declining.

The analyst works with finance, sales, and operations to define:

* Revenue by product.
* Gross margin.
* Return rate.
* Cost per shipment.
* Customer acquisition cost.
* Inventory holding cost.

The analyst also checks whether the calculations use consistent definitions and reliable data.

A dashboard is useful only when its information supports actual decisions.

## 4. The Information Technology Perspective

The Information Technology (IT) perspective examines business analysis in the context of technology-enabled change.

It may involve applications, infrastructure, cloud platforms, cybersecurity controls, integrations, and operational services.

### Business analysis considerations

* Business and technical requirements.
* System interfaces.
* Data migration.
* Availability and performance.
* Security and privacy.
* Scalability.
* Maintainability.
* Technical constraints.
* Operational support.
* Integration dependencies.

### Example

An organization migrates a business application to the cloud.

The analyst investigates:

* Business objectives for migration.
* Current application dependencies.
* Availability requirements.
* Data residency obligations.
* Recovery objectives.
* User access requirements.
* Migration risks.
* Operational support capabilities.
* Expected cost and performance improvements.

The analyst connects business needs to technical requirements without assuming that the most advanced technology is automatically the best solution.

## 5. The Business Architecture Perspective

Business Architecture examines how an organization is structured to achieve its strategy.

It helps connect strategic objectives to capabilities, value streams, information, stakeholders, and organizational structures.

### Important concepts

**Business capability:** What an organization must be able to do to achieve its objectives.

Examples:

* Customer relationship management.
* Supplier management.
* Inventory management.
* Financial reporting.

**Value stream:** A sequence of activities that delivers value to a stakeholder.

Example: Customer order-to-delivery.

**Organizational structure:** The arrangement of roles, departments, reporting relationships, and responsibilities.

**Business architecture:** A structured representation of important business elements and their relationships.

### Example

A retailer wants to expand into online sales.

The analyst assesses capabilities such as:

* Product information management.
* Digital marketing.
* Order management.
* Payment processing.
* Fulfillment.
* Returns.
* Customer support.

The assessment may reveal that the organization has strong marketing capabilities but insufficient fulfillment and returns capabilities.

The change strategy should address those gaps rather than focus exclusively on launching a website.

## 6. The Business Process Management Perspective

Business Process Management (BPM) focuses on understanding, analyzing, improving, and managing business processes.

A process transforms inputs into outputs through activities performed by people, systems, or both.

### Process analysis considers

* Process objectives.
* Inputs and outputs.
* Roles and responsibilities.
* Activities and decisions.
* Handoffs.
* Exceptions.
* Performance measures.
* Bottlenecks.
* Controls.
* Improvement opportunities.

### Example

An insurance company takes ten days to process claims.

The analyst maps the process and discovers:

* Documents are checked manually.
* Claims move between several departments.
* Missing information is identified late.
* Approval responsibilities are unclear.

Potential improvements include:

* Early validation.
* Simplified handoffs.
* Clearer decision rules.
* Automated notifications.
* Better exception handling.

Automation should follow an understanding of the process. Automating an inefficient process can preserve or amplify existing problems.

## 7. Comparing the Five Perspectives

| Perspective                 | Primary Focus                            | Typical Value                              |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Agile                       | Iterative delivery and feedback          | Faster learning and incremental value      |
| Business Intelligence       | Data and decision support                | Better-informed decisions                  |
| Information Technology      | Technology-enabled change                | Effective and reliable technical solutions |
| Business Architecture       | Organizational capabilities and strategy | Strategic alignment                        |
| Business Process Management | Process performance and improvement      | Greater efficiency and effectiveness       |

### Combining perspectives

Consider a bank introducing a new digital lending service.

* Business Architecture identifies the capabilities needed to deliver lending services.
* BPM examines the application and approval process.
* BI defines measures for approval times, default rates, and application conversion.
* IT addresses application integration, security, and availability.
* Agile supports iterative development and stakeholder feedback.

The perspectives complement one another.

## 8. Selecting a Perspective

The analyst should identify the primary context of the business need.

Ask:

1. Is the primary challenge strategic alignment or capability gaps?
2. Is the organization trying to improve a process?
3. Does the need involve data and decision support?
4. Is the change primarily technology-enabled?
5. Is iterative delivery central to the approach?

Several answers may apply simultaneously.

## 9. Practical Case Study: Retail Transformation

A retailer wants to reduce order delays and improve online customer satisfaction.

### Analysis by perspective

**Business Architecture:** Assess order management, fulfillment, inventory, and customer service capabilities.

**BPM:** Map order placement, payment, picking, packing, dispatch, and returns.

**BI:** Establish metrics for delivery time, stockouts, cancellation rate, and customer satisfaction.

**IT:** Evaluate inventory integrations, tracking systems, and system availability.

**Agile:** Deliver improvements incrementally and measure results after each release.

### Recommended approach

Establish a shared business objective, define baseline performance, prioritize the highest-impact bottlenecks, and coordinate the five perspectives as needed.

## 10. Knowledge Check

**Question 1:** Which perspective primarily focuses on organizational capabilities and their alignment with strategy?

A. Business Intelligence
B. Business Architecture
C. Agile
D. IT operations only

**Question 2:** An organization needs reliable dashboards to monitor profit margins. Which perspective is most directly relevant?

A. Business Intelligence
B. Business Process Management only
C. Agile only
D. Business Architecture only

**Question 3:** Which perspective is most directly concerned with process bottlenecks and handoffs?

A. Business Intelligence
B. Business Process Management
C. Business Architecture only
D. Financial accounting only

**Question 4:** What does Agile primarily emphasize?

A. Fixed requirements that can never change
B. Iterative delivery, feedback, and adaptation
C. Eliminating all documentation
D. Avoiding stakeholder participation

**Question 5:** Which statement is correct?

A. Only one business analysis perspective can be used in a change.
B. Business analysis perspectives may complement one another.
C. IT solutions always create business value.
D. Process automation should always occur before process analysis.

### Answers and Explanations

1. **B.** Business Architecture connects organizational structure and capabilities to strategy.
2. **A.** BI focuses on data, information, metrics, and decision support.
3. **B.** BPM focuses on analyzing and improving processes.
4. **B.** Agile emphasizes iterative delivery and learning.
5. **B.** A complex change often requires multiple perspectives.

## 11. Revision Summary

* Agile emphasizes iterative delivery and continuous feedback.
* Business Intelligence turns data into decision-support information.
* IT addresses technology-enabled change and technical constraints.
* Business Architecture connects strategy with organizational capabilities.
* BPM focuses on process performance and improvement.
* Multiple perspectives can be combined within one initiative.
* Perspective selection depends on the business context and desired value.
