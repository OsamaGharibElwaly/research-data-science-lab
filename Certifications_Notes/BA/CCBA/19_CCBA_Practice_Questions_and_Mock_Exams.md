# CCBA Practice Questions and Mock Exams

## 1. Purpose

This module provides original practice questions to reinforce business analysis concepts relevant to CCBA preparation.

The questions are independently written study exercises. They are not official IIBA exam questions and do not reproduce confidential examination content.

Use them to identify weaknesses, practice scenario-based reasoning, and review explanations.

## 2. Recommended Practice Method

For each question:

1. Read the entire scenario.
2. Identify the business need or problem.
3. Determine what the analyst should accomplish.
4. Eliminate options that jump to conclusions.
5. Select the best answer.
6. Read the explanation.
7. Record the topic if you answered incorrectly.

Suggested scoring interpretation for this practice set:

* 90–100%: Strong performance on this set; continue with mixed scenarios.
* 80–89%: Good progress; review incorrect answers.
* 70–79%: Revisit weak knowledge areas and repeat practice.
* Below 70%: Review core concepts before attempting the questions again.

These are personal study guidelines, not official CCBA passing-score thresholds.

# Part A: Knowledge Area Questions

## Question 1 — Business Analysis Planning

A business analyst is assigned to a project involving a new customer service platform. The organization has strict regulatory obligations, multiple stakeholder groups, and an established approval process.

What should the analyst consider first when determining the business analysis approach?

A. Use the same approach used on the analyst's previous project.

B. Understand the initiative's context, complexity, governance, stakeholders, and constraints.

C. Begin writing functional requirements immediately.

D. Allow the development team to determine all business analysis activities.

**Correct answer: B**

**Explanation:** The approach should fit the context, risk, complexity, governance, and stakeholder environment. A previous approach may provide useful ideas but should not be copied without evaluation.

## Question 2 — Elicitation

During an interview, a department manager requests an automated approval feature. The manager cannot clearly explain why existing approvals are insufficient.

What should the analyst do?

A. Record the feature exactly as requested and close the discussion.

B. Immediately recommend a different approval platform.

C. Ask questions to understand the underlying problem, current process, and expected outcome.

D. Reject the request because the manager cannot explain it.

**Correct answer: C**

**Explanation:** The analyst should investigate the need behind the proposed solution. Clarifying the problem helps determine whether automation is appropriate.

## Question 3 — Requirements Life Cycle Management

A developer discovers that a proposed change affects three approved requirements and two existing test cases.

What should the analyst do first?

A. Allow the developer to implement the change without documentation.

B. Assess the change's impacts, dependencies, and implications using the applicable process.

C. Delete the existing requirements.

D. Reject the change automatically.

**Correct answer: B**

**Explanation:** Impact analysis supports informed decisions. The change may be beneficial, but its implications must be understood before authorization and implementation.

## Question 4 — Strategy Analysis

An organization wants to replace its inventory software because stock discrepancies are increasing.

What is the best initial analytical action?

A. Purchase the most popular inventory system.

B. Investigate the current process, discrepancy patterns, causes, and business objectives.

C. Begin migrating inventory records.

D. Select a vendor based on presentation quality.

**Correct answer: B**

**Explanation:** Current-state and root-cause analysis help determine whether software replacement is justified or whether other process, data, or control issues are responsible.

## Question 5 — Requirements Analysis

Which requirement is the most measurable?

A. The system should be easy to use.

B. The system should process transactions quickly.

C. The system shall process at least 100 transactions per minute under the agreed test conditions.

D. The system should have an attractive interface.

**Correct answer: C**

**Explanation:** It defines a measurable performance threshold and indicates that test conditions must be agreed.

## Question 6 — Solution Evaluation

A new reporting system is delivered on time, but employees continue using spreadsheets and management reports remain delayed.

What should the analyst investigate?

A. Only whether the project met its delivery deadline.

B. Adoption, workflow fit, data quality, training, and the causes of continued delays.

C. Whether the interface uses modern colors.

D. Whether additional features can be added immediately.

**Correct answer: B**

**Explanation:** Delivery does not guarantee value. The analyst should investigate the reasons the solution has not achieved its intended outcome.

# Part B: Scenario-Based Questions

## Question 7 — Stakeholder Conflict

Finance wants strict purchasing approvals. Operations wants faster purchasing. Compliance requires auditable decisions.

What is the most appropriate approach?

A. Implement the finance department's request because it controls the budget.

B. Follow operations because speed is the main business concern.

C. Clarify the underlying needs, constraints, and risks, then facilitate evaluation of alternatives.

D. Allow each department to implement a separate process without coordination.

**Correct answer: C**

**Explanation:** The analyst should understand the competing interests and support a solution that considers business value, control requirements, and operational efficiency.

## Question 8 — Requirement Validation

A requirement has a unique identifier, clear wording, measurable acceptance criteria, and no apparent contradictions. However, users say it does not solve their problem.

Which issue is most likely present?

A. Verification failure only.

B. Validation failure.

C. Version-control failure only.

D. Requirements have no value.

**Correct answer: B**

**Explanation:** The requirement may be well-formed but still fail to represent the actual stakeholder need.

## Question 9 — Change Impact

A company adds a new customer-data field to its CRM system.

Which consideration is most likely to be overlooked if the analyst focuses only on the screen design?

A. The color of the company logo.

B. Database changes, integrations, data validation, privacy, migration, and testing.

C. The project title.

D. The number of meeting invitations.

**Correct answer: B**

**Explanation:** A small visible change may affect multiple components and operational obligations.

## Question 10 — Traceability

A test case has been created, but the team cannot identify which requirement or business objective justifies it.

What should the analyst investigate?

A. Whether the test can be deleted immediately.

B. Whether the test has a valid requirement relationship or represents a legitimate additional control.

C. Whether all requirements should be renamed.

D. Whether the development team should ignore traceability.

**Correct answer: B**

**Explanation:** Traceability helps identify orphaned tests, missing requirements, and legitimate controls that may require documentation.

## Question 11 — Risk

A proposed integration has a low probability of failure but could expose sensitive customer information if it fails.

What should the analyst do?

A. Ignore the risk because the probability is low.

B. Assess the potential impact, applicable obligations, and suitable risk responses.

C. Approve the integration automatically.

D. Remove security requirements to reduce implementation effort.

**Correct answer: B**

**Explanation:** Low probability does not necessarily mean low risk. The severity of potential consequences and applicable controls matter.

## Question 12 — Ethics

A project sponsor asks the analyst to report only favorable results from a pilot because the executive committee is considering additional funding.

What should the analyst do?

A. Remove unfavorable findings.

B. Present the evidence accurately and explain material limitations and risks.

C. Invent stronger performance figures.

D. Delay all reporting indefinitely.

**Correct answer: B**

**Explanation:** Honest, evidence-based reporting supports responsible decisions. Material findings should not be concealed to mislead decision-makers.

# Part C: Applied Analysis Questions

## Question 13 — Current State and Future State

A warehouse processes orders manually and frequently experiences picking errors. Management wants automated barcode scanning.

Which sequence is most appropriate?

A. Purchase scanners, deploy them, and then investigate the problem.

B. Understand current processes and error causes, define desired outcomes, assess gaps, and evaluate change options.

C. Replace every warehouse system immediately.

D. Focus only on hardware cost.

**Correct answer: B**

**Explanation:** Strategy analysis establishes the need, current state, future state, and change strategy before selecting an appropriate option.

## Question 14 — Prioritization

A project has limited resources. One requirement is legally mandatory, another reduces a major operational risk, and a third adds a cosmetic feature.

What is the best approach?

A. Prioritize the cosmetic feature because it is easiest.

B. Prioritize only according to stakeholder seniority.

C. Evaluate regulatory obligations, business value, risks, dependencies, and delivery constraints.

D. Give all requirements equal priority.

**Correct answer: C**

**Explanation:** Prioritization should reflect value, risk, obligations, and dependencies. A mandatory requirement may be a hard constraint rather than an ordinary trade-off.

## Question 15 — Performance Metrics

A team reports that average processing time improved after automation. However, the new measurement excludes complex cases that were included in the old measurement.

What should the analyst do?

A. Declare success based on the new average.

B. Compare results without considering the difference.

C. Investigate the measurement definitions and establish a fair comparison.

D. Stop measuring processing time.

**Correct answer: C**

**Explanation:** Performance comparisons require consistent definitions or a clear adjustment for changes in scope and data collection.

## Question 16 — Solution Design

A stakeholder specifies that the business must be able to view monthly sales by region. No technology constraint has been established.

What should the analyst do?

A. Require a specific database vendor immediately.

B. Clarify the information need and evaluate appropriate design options.

C. Reject the requirement because it does not specify a database.

D. Begin coding without clarifying reporting needs.

**Correct answer: B**

**Explanation:** The business need should be understood before selecting a specific implementation, unless a legitimate constraint already determines the technology.

## 3. Weak-Area Procedure

For every incorrect answer:

1. Record the question number.
2. Identify the concept you misunderstood.
3. Write why your selected answer was incorrect.
4. Write why the correct answer is better.
5. Review the relevant study module.
6. Create a new example of the same concept.
7. Reattempt the question later without viewing the answer.

Do not memorize answer letters. The goal is to understand the reasoning that makes an answer appropriate in its context.
