# 18. ESEP Application and Professional Contributions

## 1. Purpose

This module provides a structured approach to preparing an ESEP application and documenting professional contributions to systems engineering. It covers application planning, experience records, professional references, contributions to the discipline, and evidence quality.

ESEP requirements, forms, fees, reference rules, and review procedures can change. Use the current official INCOSE certification guidance as the authority for eligibility and submission requirements.

## 2. Learning Objectives

* Organize an application around current eligibility requirements.
* Maintain a complete and traceable professional experience history.
* Distinguish professional contributions from ordinary job responsibilities.
* Document contributions to engineering practice, education, leadership, and knowledge sharing.
* Prepare references who can substantiate relevant experience.
* Track application progress and resolve missing evidence.
* Avoid unsupported claims and incomplete submissions.

## 3. Understanding the Application

### 3.1 Application Readiness

Before preparing the final submission, confirm:

* Current ESEP eligibility criteria.
* Required education and experience evidence.
* Applicable systems engineering experience expectations.
* Required certification or knowledge prerequisites, if any.
* Professional reference requirements.
* Required descriptions of leadership and professional contributions.
* Application fee and submission method.
* Review, interview, or additional assessment procedures.
* Renewal or continuing professional development obligations, where applicable.

Do not assume that eligibility requirements or forms are identical to ASEP or CSEP.

### 3.2 Application Readiness Categories

Use four categories to organize your progress:

**Confirmed:** Verified against the current official requirements.

**Documented:** Supporting records have been collected.

**Pending:** A record, response, or clarification is outstanding.

**Needs review:** Information exists but must be checked for completeness, accuracy, or relevance.

This separation helps distinguish an eligibility requirement that has been met from one that is merely being prepared.

## 4. Professional Experience Records

### 4.1 Experience Inventory

For each relevant position or project, record:

* Employer or organization.
* Position and dates.
* Project or system context.
* Lifecycle phases involved.
* Systems engineering responsibilities.
* Technical decisions and leadership contributions.
* Stakeholders and teams coordinated.
* Supporting evidence.
* Potential professional references.

Follow the official application's method for calculating experience. Do not independently count overlapping roles or academic activities in ways that conflict with the stated eligibility rules.

### 4.2 Experience Description Structure

A useful description should answer five questions:

1. What system or engineering problem was involved?
2. What responsibility did you personally hold?
3. Which systems engineering processes or methods did you apply?
4. What decisions or actions did you take?
5. What outcomes or evidence resulted?

Focus on the substance of the work instead of copying a job description.

### 4.3 Experience and Competency Mapping

| Experience area             | Questions to answer                                     | Possible supporting evidence     |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Requirements                | How were needs analyzed and requirements managed?       | Requirements records             |
| Architecture                | How were system structures and alternatives evaluated?  | Architecture or decision records |
| Interfaces                  | How were cross-system dependencies managed?             | Interface specifications         |
| Integration                 | How was compatibility demonstrated?                     | Integration reports              |
| Verification and validation | How was requirement satisfaction established?           | Test plans and reports           |
| Risk                        | How were technical uncertainties addressed?             | Risk registers                   |
| Leadership                  | How were technical decisions coordinated or influenced? | Review records and references    |
| Lifecycle management        | How were changes and operational needs handled?         | Change and lifecycle records     |

These are evidence prompts, not a substitute for any official ESEP experience categories.

## 5. Professional Contributions

### 5.1 What Is a Professional Contribution?

A professional contribution is an activity that helps develop, improve, communicate, or advance systems engineering knowledge or practice beyond simply performing an assigned task.

Possible categories include:

* Technical publications and professional articles.
* Conference presentations and technical workshops.
* Contributions to professional societies or working groups.
* Mentoring and developing other engineers.
* Teaching or delivering professional training.
* Developing reusable methods, templates, or guidance.
* Leading improvements to organizational engineering processes.
* Participating in standards or handbook development.
* Sharing lessons learned and validated engineering practices.
* Supporting professional communities and knowledge exchange.

These examples are not guaranteed to satisfy a particular application criterion. Describe the contribution accurately and verify the current requirements.

### 5.2 Contribution Versus Routine Responsibility

Routine responsibility is the work assigned as part of a role.

A professional contribution demonstrates an additional benefit, such as improving a method used by several teams, teaching others a reusable technique, or sharing validated lessons with the professional community.

For example:

* Routine responsibility: Preparing a project requirements document.
* Potential contribution: Developing a reusable requirements-review method adopted by several project teams.

The distinction depends on the actual scope and impact, not the wording alone.

### 5.3 Contribution Assessment

For each contribution, document:

**Purpose:** What problem or need did it address?

**Personal role:** What did you create, lead, organize, or improve?

**Audience:** Who benefited from the work?

**Scope:** Was the impact individual, team-wide, organizational, or professional-community-wide?

**Evidence:** What record confirms the contribution?

**Outcome:** What changed or improved?

**Continuity:** Was the contribution sustained, reused, or expanded?

Avoid claiming an organizational or industry-wide impact unless the available evidence supports that scope.

## 6. Contribution Evidence Examples

### 6.1 Mentoring

Potential evidence includes a mentoring plan, training materials, documented sessions, feedback, and examples of capability development.

Describe what you taught, how you supported learning, and what evidence demonstrates the outcome.

### 6.2 Process Improvement

Potential evidence includes the original process, the problem identified, the revised method, approval records, adoption by teams, and measured outcomes.

Distinguish between proposing an improvement, implementing it, and demonstrating that it was effective.

### 6.3 Technical Knowledge Sharing

Potential evidence includes published articles, recorded presentations, workshop materials, technical notes, and attendance or feedback records where appropriate.

Clearly distinguish published material from internal documentation and distinguish sole authorship from collaborative contributions.

### 6.4 Professional Society Participation

Potential evidence may include formal participation records, working-group responsibilities, contributions to a deliverable, event organization, or documented service.

Membership alone should not be represented as leadership or a substantive contribution.

## 7. Professional References

### 7.1 Selecting References

Choose people who can credibly verify the experience or contributions attributed to them.

Consider whether a prospective reference:

* Worked with you directly.
* Observed the relevant engineering activities.
* Understands your actual responsibility.
* Can explain the technical context.
* Can confirm the dates and scope of your work.
* Is willing and available to respond.
* Meets the current application's reference rules.

Follow official requirements for reference qualifications and independence. Do not assume that any colleague, friend, or family member will qualify.

### 7.2 Preparing References Ethically

You may provide a factual summary of your role, dates, and relevant projects to help a reference recall the work.

However:

* Do not write or submit a reference as if you were the referee.
* Do not pressure someone to endorse an inaccurate claim.
* Do not imply that a person has agreed before receiving confirmation.
* Do not provide confidential application materials without authorization.
* Allow references to express their own professional judgment.

### 7.3 Reference Tracking

Record the reference's name, professional relationship, relevant experience, permission to contact them, submission status, and follow-up date.

Store contact details securely and share them only as needed for the application.

## 8. Application Writing Quality

Strong application descriptions are:

* Specific and factual.
* Focused on personal contribution.
* Consistent with supporting records.
* Clear about the system and technical context.
* Honest about authority and limitations.
* Supported by outcomes where available.
* Written in language that a technical reviewer can understand.

Avoid vague statements such as "responsible for all engineering" when your actual responsibility covered a specific subsystem or activity.

Avoid unexplained acronyms, excessive tool lists, inflated titles, and claims that cannot be verified.

## 9. Application Workflow

1. Download the current official application instructions.
2. Extract all eligibility and evidence requirements.
3. Create a requirement-by-requirement checklist.
4. Prepare the professional experience inventory.
5. Map relevant experience to the required categories.
6. Collect and review supporting records.
7. Identify professional contributions and document their impact.
8. Confirm appropriate references and their availability.
9. Check dates, titles, project descriptions, and consistency.
10. Obtain any necessary organizational permissions.
11. Complete the required forms.
12. Conduct a final completeness and accuracy review.
13. Submit through the official process.
14. Track acknowledgements, requests for clarification, and subsequent decisions.

## 10. Exercises

### Exercise 1: Professional Contribution

You created a checklist used only by yourself to review system requirements. What additional evidence would help determine whether this is a broader professional contribution?

### Exercise 2: Experience Evidence

An experience description states, "Led system architecture," but the candidate only prepared alternatives for approval by a chief engineer. How should the description be revised?

### Exercise 3: Reference Readiness

A prospective reference knows the candidate well but did not work on the project being described. What should the candidate verify before nominating that person?

## 11. Suggested Answers

**Exercise 1:** Determine whether the checklist was shared, adopted by other engineers, improved a repeatable practice, or generated documented benefits. If it was only personal, describe it honestly as an individual work method rather than claiming wider organizational impact.

**Exercise 2:** State that the candidate developed and evaluated architecture alternatives and provided a recommendation to the authorized decision-maker. Claiming leadership of the final architecture decision would misrepresent the authority held.

**Exercise 3:** Check the official reference requirements and determine whether the person can credibly verify the specific experience. If they did not observe the relevant work, select a more suitable reference where possible.

## 12. Key Takeaways

* The official application instructions determine eligibility and evidence requirements.
* Professional experience descriptions should explain actual responsibility and engineering contribution.
* Professional contributions should have a clear purpose, audience, evidence, and outcome.
* Mentoring, process improvement, and knowledge sharing can be meaningful contributions when properly substantiated.
* References must be suitable, willing, and able to verify the relevant experience.
* A complete application is accurate, consistent, authorized, and traceable.
