# 13 — CSEP Application and References

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

* Explain the main stages of the INCOSE CSEP certification process.
* Identify the difference between knowledge requirements and experience requirements.
* Prepare an accurate systems engineering experience inventory.
* Select suitable professional references.
* Explain how reference coverage should map to claimed experience.
* Prepare evidence that supports application statements.
* Identify common application mistakes and avoid unsupported claims.
* Develop a practical application-readiness plan.

> **Important:** This module reflects publicly available INCOSE guidance reviewed in October 2026. Certification requirements, forms, fees, portals, and procedures can change. The official INCOSE certification pages and current program requirements take precedence over this study guide.

## 2. What Is CSEP?

The Certified Systems Engineering Professional (CSEP) is an INCOSE professional certification for individuals who demonstrate systems engineering knowledge and the required professional experience.

CSEP is not simply a course-completion certificate. The certification process considers both knowledge and professional experience, together with the required application and experience confirmation.

The main elements are:

1. Meet the knowledge requirement through an approved pathway.
2. Meet the applicable education and professional experience requirements.
3. Document systems engineering experience in the application.
4. Obtain appropriate professional references to confirm the claimed experience.
5. Complete the required application, review, and certification steps.

The exact pathway and requirements depend on the candidate's qualifications and the current INCOSE program rules.

## 3. Understand Eligibility Before Applying

### 3.1 Knowledge requirement

The standard knowledge pathway involves the INCOSE Systems Engineering knowledge examination. An approved academic-equivalency pathway may be available to eligible candidates through recognized programs.

Do not assume that completing a private training course, reading a handbook, or passing a third-party practice test automatically satisfies the official knowledge requirement.

Official information:

* CSEP application: https://www.incose.org/certification/start-your-certification/applying-for-csep/
* Taking the exam: https://www.incose.org/certification/start-your-certification/taking-the-exam/
* Academic equivalency: https://www.incose.org/certification/academic-equivalency/

### 3.2 Professional experience requirement

Under the published CSEP requirements for a candidate with a qualifying degree, the candidate needs at least **five years, or 60 months, of systems engineering experience**.

The published experience-distribution guidance also requires at least one year of experience in each of at least three of the defined systems engineering experience areas.

For candidates without a qualifying degree, different requirements apply. Do not use the qualifying-degree route as a universal rule.

The current INCOSE requirements document and application guidance determine which experience qualifies.

### 3.3 Why the distinction matters

These are different questions:

* **Knowledge:** Do you meet the prescribed systems engineering knowledge requirement?
* **Experience:** Have you performed the required systems engineering work for the required duration?
* **Confirmation:** Can appropriate references substantiate the experience claimed?
* **Application compliance:** Have you completed the prescribed application and review process?

Meeting one does not automatically satisfy the others.

## 4. The Main CSEP Application Workflow

The following workflow summarizes the standard process. Follow the current portal instructions for the exact order and timing.

### Step 1 — Create or access your INCOSE profile

Create an INCOSE account or access your existing profile. Ensure your personal and professional details are accurate.

### Step 2 — Review the current eligibility criteria

Check:

* Education and qualifying-degree rules
* Required experience duration
* Systems engineering experience areas
* Knowledge examination or approved alternative pathway
* Reference requirements
* Application validity period
* Fees and renewal requirements

Do this before paying an application fee.

### Step 3 — Plan your knowledge pathway

Determine whether you will take the knowledge examination or whether an approved alternative pathway applies to your circumstances.

If you are taking the exam, use the current exam guidance to understand the format, timing, permitted materials, and scheduling process.

### Step 4 — Prepare your experience inventory

Document each relevant professional role and project. Record dates, responsibilities, systems engineering activities, outcomes, and evidence.

Do not wait until the application form is open to reconstruct several years of experience from memory.

### Step 5 — Identify and contact references

Choose people who directly know your systems engineering work and can confirm the period and activities they observed.

Ask whether they are willing and able to provide an accurate reference before entering their details into the application.

### Step 6 — Complete the application

Enter your experience accurately and consistently. Ensure that the claimed durations and descriptions match the evidence and the information your references can confirm.

### Step 7 — Submit the application and follow the portal process

Follow the current INCOSE portal instructions for submission, payment, and reference requests.

The reference workflow and portal steps may change, so do not rely on screenshots or instructions for an older application system.

### Step 8 — Monitor reference completion and review

Check the status of references and any requests for clarification. The review may be delayed if references are missing or if claimed experience requires additional explanation.

### Step 9 — Complete outstanding certification requirements

Ensure the knowledge requirement, experience review, and other applicable steps are completed within the required time period.

## 5. Build an Application-Ready Experience Inventory

Before writing application narratives, build a single source of truth for your professional history.

Recommended fields:

| Field                     | What to record                                                         |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Organization              | Employer, client, or project sponsor                                   |
| Position                  | Actual job title                                                       |
| Start and end dates       | Month and year, where requested                                        |
| Employment status         | Full-time, part-time, internship, contract, or other applicable status |
| Project                   | System or project name                                                 |
| System context            | Purpose, users, operational environment, and boundaries                |
| Personal responsibility   | What you personally did                                                |
| Systems engineering areas | Relevant areas supported by the work                                   |
| Deliverables              | Requirements, analyses, designs, plans, test records, or other outputs |
| Outcomes                  | Results you can support                                                |
| Reference                 | Person who directly knows the work                                     |
| Evidence                  | Authorized records that support the description                        |

### Keep dates consistent

Use the same dates across your application, CV, employment history, and reference information.

If a project overlaps with another role, document the overlap honestly. Do not automatically add overlapping months together as if they were separate elapsed time.

Do not assume that every month of employment qualifies as a month of systems engineering experience. The work itself and the current INCOSE criteria determine what can be claimed.

## 6. Describe Experience in Systems Engineering Terms

An application should describe the engineering work performed, not just the job title.

### Weak description

“Worked as an IT engineer and configured networks.”

This provides little evidence of systems engineering activity.

### Stronger description, if accurate

“Supported network-service migration by documenting interface requirements, analyzing service dependencies, coordinating configuration changes, and executing requirement-based acceptance tests.”

The stronger statement identifies activities that may be relevant to systems engineering.

However, it is only appropriate if those activities were actually performed. Do not add requirements analysis, risk management, or verification to a description simply because the terms sound relevant.

### A useful structure

For each experience entry, explain:

1. **Context:** What system or project was involved?
2. **Responsibility:** What was your role?
3. **Activity:** What systems engineering work did you perform?
4. **Method:** How did you carry it out?
5. **Result:** What was delivered or improved?
6. **Evidence:** What can confirm the work?

This structure makes your experience easier to understand and verify.

## 7. Systems Engineering Experience Areas

INCOSE publishes a defined set of systems engineering experience areas. Use the current official list and its definitions when preparing your application.

Examples of relevant activities include:

* Business or mission analysis
* Stakeholder needs and requirements definition
* System requirements definition
* Architecture definition
* Design definition
* System analysis
* Implementation
* Integration
* Verification
* Validation
* Transition
* Operation
* Maintenance
* Disposal
* Technical planning, assessment, control, and other applicable systems engineering management activities

The exact wording and classification should be checked against the current program documents.

### Example: Mapping work to experience areas

Suppose an engineer helped migrate a network service.

Possible activities include:

* Gathering service needs and constraints
* Documenting interfaces and dependencies
* Evaluating alternative network configurations
* Planning the migration
* Coordinating component integration
* Testing requirements after migration
* Evaluating operational performance

Only the activities actually performed should be included. Routine configuration or troubleshooting does not automatically demonstrate all these areas.

## 8. Choosing Professional References

References are not merely people who can say that you are a good employee. They need to be able to confirm relevant experience.

INCOSE's published reference guidance states that a reference should have known the candidate during the claimed period and been aware of the candidate's work as it occurred. The reference should also be sufficiently familiar with systems engineering to describe the work.

Potential references may include:

* Current or former supervisors
* Technical leads
* Project managers who directly observed your work
* Customers or clients with direct knowledge of the relevant activities
* Other appropriate professionals who can credibly verify your experience

The best reference is not necessarily the most senior person. It is someone who can accurately describe the work they observed.

### Reference suitability checklist

Before selecting a reference, ask:

* Did this person know me during the claimed experience period?
* Did they directly observe or have sufficient knowledge of my work?
* Can they describe the systems engineering activities I performed?
* Can they confirm the relevant dates and responsibilities?
* Are they willing to complete the prescribed INCOSE reference process?
* Can they respond to questions about the experience if needed?

A reference who knows you personally but has never observed your work is generally a poor choice for confirming professional experience.

## 9. Reference Coverage: The Important Principle

For CSEP, references collectively need to confirm the required systems engineering experience.

One reference may be able to confirm a large portion of the experience. Other references may cover additional periods or projects.

The important point is **coverage of the required experience**, not simply the number of names submitted.

For example, suppose a candidate claims five years of relevant experience:

| Reference   | Period directly known | Example of contribution                          |
| ----------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Reference A | Years 1–2             | Confirms project activities and responsibilities |
| Reference B | Years 2–4             | Confirms work across several projects            |
| Reference C | Years 4–5             | Confirms later role and technical activities     |

This is only an illustrative coverage model. Actual eligibility depends on the periods, work, reference suitability, and current INCOSE rules.

### Avoid gaps and unsupported periods

A common problem occurs when several references confirm the same two years but no reference can confirm the remaining claimed experience.

Before applying, create a coverage table:

| Claimed period | Relevant work | Reference who can confirm it | Status     |
| -------------- | ------------- | ---------------------------- | ---------- |
| Period 1       | Project A     | Reference A                  | To confirm |
| Period 2       | Project B     | Reference B                  | To confirm |
| Period 3       | Project C     | Reference C                  | To confirm |

Ensure the coverage is accurate and does not double-count overlapping periods.

## 10. Communicating with References Professionally

Contact potential references before initiating formal requests.

Explain:

* That you intend to apply for CSEP.
* Which roles or projects you believe they can confirm.
* The approximate dates involved.
* The types of systems engineering activities they observed.
* That they should provide an independent and truthful assessment.
* That they may receive a formal request through the INCOSE process.

Do not ask a reference to endorse activities they did not observe or to confirm dates they cannot verify.

### Sample email

**Subject:** Request to Serve as an INCOSE CSEP Reference

Dear [Name],

I am preparing an application for the INCOSE Certified Systems Engineering Professional (CSEP) certification and would like to ask whether you would be comfortable serving as a professional reference for the work we were involved in together.

I intend to document my responsibilities during [project or role] from approximately [start date] to [end date], particularly the systems engineering activities that you directly observed.

INCOSE uses a prescribed reference process, and I would provide the relevant instructions if you agree. I would appreciate an accurate assessment of my work and understand if you are unable to confirm any part of the experience.

Thank you for considering my request.

Best regards,
[Your Name]

## 11. What If You Have Limited References?

If you cannot find someone who can confirm a particular period, do not invent a reference or rely on a generic recommendation letter.

Instead:

1. Review your previous supervisors, project leads, and clients.
2. Identify who directly observed the work during the missing period.
3. Check whether another suitable person can independently confirm that work.
4. Determine whether the relevant experience can be documented and verified.
5. Contact INCOSE certification support if you are unsure how to handle the gap.

INCOSE warns that experience may not receive credit if it cannot be adequately confirmed. Missing reference coverage can delay or jeopardize the application.

A reference should not be selected only because they have an impressive title or are an INCOSE member. The ability to confirm the relevant work is central.

## 12. Evidence and Confidentiality

Useful supporting records may include:

* Requirements documents
* Architecture and interface artifacts
* Technical plans
* Risk registers
* Trade-study reports
* Verification and validation plans
* Test results
* Technical review records
* Change and configuration records
* Maintenance or operational records
* Supervisor confirmation

Do not upload or share confidential company information unless authorized. Do not disclose passwords, customer data, proprietary designs, restricted technical information, or sensitive personal information.

A concise description and a suitable reference may be more appropriate than sharing a confidential project artifact.

## 13. Common Application Mistakes

### Mistake 1 — Treating every technical job as systems engineering

**Correction:** Describe the actual work and map it to the current experience-area definitions.

### Mistake 2 — Using a job title as proof

**Correction:** Explain the activities, decisions, deliverables, and results.

### Mistake 3 — Counting overlapping periods twice

**Correction:** Calculate elapsed time consistently and disclose overlaps.

### Mistake 4 — Choosing references who cannot confirm the work

**Correction:** Prioritize people who directly observed the claimed experience.

### Mistake 5 — Submitting generic recommendation letters

**Correction:** Follow the prescribed INCOSE reference process.

### Mistake 6 — Exaggerating responsibility

**Correction:** Distinguish what you observed, assisted with, contributed to, developed, coordinated, or led.

### Mistake 7 — Waiting until the deadline to request references

**Correction:** Identify references early and allow time for them to respond.

### Mistake 8 — Using outdated instructions

**Correction:** Check the current INCOSE portal and program requirements before submission.

### Mistake 9 — Assuming passing the examination completes certification

**Correction:** Complete the applicable experience review, reference, and application requirements as well.

### Mistake 10 — Assuming a self-assessment establishes eligibility

**Correction:** Use the official criteria and review process.

## 15. Exercises with Suggested Answers

### Exercise 1 — Experience eligibility

A candidate has five years of IT employment but has only performed routine support tasks.

Does the job duration alone establish CSEP eligibility?

**Suggested answer:** No. The candidate must demonstrate qualifying systems engineering experience under the current INCOSE rules. Employment duration alone is insufficient.

### Exercise 2 — Reference selection

A senior executive knows the candidate socially but did not observe the candidate's technical work. A project supervisor directly observed three years of relevant work.

Which person is more suitable to confirm those three years?

**Suggested answer:** The project supervisor is generally more suitable because they directly observed the relevant work and can confirm the activities and dates.

### Exercise 3 — Reference coverage

Three references each confirm the same two-year period. The candidate claims five years of experience.

What should the candidate investigate?

**Suggested answer:** The candidate should identify who can confirm the remaining period and ensure that the combined reference evidence covers the required experience without double-counting.

### Exercise 4 — Experience statement

A candidate writes, “I led system validation,” but actually executed test cases prepared by another engineer.

What is a more accurate description?

**Suggested answer:** “Executed defined system test cases and recorded results under the project's validation plan,” if that accurately describes the work. The candidate should not claim leadership they did not exercise.

### Exercise 5 — Missing documentation

A candidate completed relevant work several years ago but no longer has access to project documents.

What can help?

**Suggested answer:** A truthful reconstruction of the activities, available non-confidential records, and confirmation from a supervisor or other suitable reference may help. The candidate must not invent evidence.

### Exercise 6 — Generic letter

A former manager provides a general recommendation praising the candidate's work ethic.

Does this automatically satisfy the INCOSE reference requirement?

**Suggested answer:** No. The candidate should follow the current prescribed reference process.

### Exercise 7 — Conflicting dates

A candidate's CV says a project ended in June, but the application says August.

What should happen before submission?

**Suggested answer:** Verify the actual dates, correct the inconsistent records, and ensure the reference can confirm the claimed period.

### Exercise 8 — Application timing

A candidate has completed the experience inventory but has not contacted any references.

What is the next practical action?

**Suggested answer:** Identify suitable references, verify that they can confirm the relevant work, contact them, and plan for the formal reference process before submitting.

## 16. Original Exam-Style Practice Questions

*These questions are independently written for study practice and are not official INCOSE examination questions.*

### Question 1

What is the principal purpose of professional references in a CSEP application?

A. To guarantee that the candidate passes the exam
B. To confirm the candidate's claimed systems engineering experience
C. To replace the experience inventory
D. To provide a general character recommendation only

**Answer: B.** References help confirm the relevant experience and the activities performed.

### Question 2

A candidate has five years of employment but only two years of qualifying systems engineering experience. What is the best conclusion?

A. Five years of employment automatically satisfies the experience requirement.
B. The candidate should claim all five years regardless of the work performed.
C. The candidate must evaluate qualifying systems engineering experience against the current criteria.
D. The candidate needs no references.

**Answer: C.** The relevance and duration of the actual work matter.

### Question 3

Which reference is generally most appropriate for confirming a specific project period?

A. A famous person who has never worked with the candidate
B. A former supervisor who directly observed the candidate's work
C. A friend who knows the candidate personally
D. An unrelated colleague who joined after the project ended

**Answer: B.** Direct knowledge of the work makes the reference more capable of confirming it.

### Question 4

Three references confirm the same period, leaving another claimed period unconfirmed. What should the candidate do?

A. Count the overlapping period three times.
B. Ignore the missing period.
C. Find appropriate confirmation for the remaining experience and follow INCOSE guidance.
D. Ask a reference to guess.

**Answer: C.** The collective reference evidence must adequately cover the required experience.

### Question 5

Which statement best describes a defensible application narrative?

A. It uses impressive terminology even when activities were not performed.
B. It describes actual responsibilities, methods, deliverables, and outcomes.
C. It lists only job titles.
D. It excludes all dates.

**Answer: B.** An accurate and evidence-based narrative is easier to review and verify.

### Question 6

What is the best approach to confidential project evidence?

A. Upload every internal project document.
B. Share customer data with any reference.
C. Use authorized evidence and avoid disclosing confidential information.
D. Remove all technical detail from the application.

**Answer: C.** Documentation should support claims without violating confidentiality obligations.

### Question 7

Which statement about an examination pass is most accurate?

A. It always completes the entire certification process.
B. It replaces all experience requirements.
C. It satisfies the knowledge requirement associated with that examination pathway, but other applicable requirements remain.
D. It makes the reference process optional in every case.

**Answer: C.** Certification includes applicable experience and application requirements in addition to knowledge.

### Question 8

A candidate is unsure whether an internship qualifies as systems engineering experience. What is the best action?

A. Automatically count every internship month.
B. Exclude all internships without checking.
C. Compare the actual work with current INCOSE rules and seek clarification if necessary.
D. Ask a friend to decide.

**Answer: C.** Eligibility depends on the current rules and the nature of the work.

### Question 9

Why should references be contacted early?

A. To allow time for confirming experience and completing the prescribed process
B. To let them invent project results
C. To bypass the portal
D. To guarantee application approval

**Answer: A.** Early communication reduces avoidable delays.

### Question 10

Which source should take precedence if an older study document conflicts with current CSEP application instructions?

A. The older study document
B. A forum comment
C. The current official INCOSE certification requirements and instructions
D. A third-party practice question

**Answer: C.** Official current requirements govern the certification process.

## 17. Revision Notes

* CSEP requires both the applicable knowledge requirement and qualifying professional experience.
* The qualifying-degree pathway published by INCOSE specifies at least 60 months of systems engineering experience and at least one year in each of at least three experience areas.
* Different requirements may apply depending on educational qualifications.
* Describe actual systems engineering activities, not just job titles.
* References must be able to confirm the work and the claimed period.
* Combined reference coverage matters more than the number of names alone.
* Generic recommendation letters do not replace the prescribed reference process.
* Keep dates consistent and avoid double-counting overlapping periods.
* Protect confidential information.
* Verify current forms, fees, application steps, and deadlines on the official website.
